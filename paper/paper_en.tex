\title{Regime-Aware Multi-Country Tourism Demand Forecasting: Integrating Economic Signals and Cross-Country Dependencies}

\author{Nguyen-Tuan~Tran,
        Hoai-Bao~Phan}

\IEEEtitleabstractindextext{%
\begin{abstract}
Forecasting international tourism arrivals and overnight stays requires models that capture strong seasonality, adapt to macroeconomic regime disruptions, and strictly satisfy physical domain accounting constraints ($\text{Nights} \ge \text{Arrivals}$). We propose and evaluate \textbf{RA-HE-DLinear}, which integrates a decomposition-linear temporal backbone (\textbf{RA-Exo-DLinear}), causal Hidden Markov Model (HMM) regime conditioning, dual-stream exogenous projections, low-rank spatial factor modeling (TRMF), and a closed-form physical consistency projection ($\Pi_{\mathcal{C}}$). In a matched Eurostat panel of 152 destination--accommodation series across \ReviewerObservedPairs{} observed target pairs (January 2024--July 2026), minimal-parameter Seasonal Naive achieves the lowest count errors (\ReviewerSNaiveArrivalWAPE{}\% arrivals and \ReviewerSNaiveNightsWAPE{}\% nights WAPE) during the tranquil, highly seasonal post-pandemic cycle (Occam's razor). However, unconstrained statistical, machine-learning, and linear baselines produce physically invalid predictions where overnight stays fall below arrivals (up to 8.786\% violation rate in SARIMAX and 1.931\% in Vanilla DLinear), and degrade sharply during structural breaks. While an unconstrained static spatial blend suffers from post-pandemic spatial factor drift (\ReviewerSpatialArrivalWAPE{}\% and \ReviewerSpatialNightsWAPE{}\% WAPE), equipping RA-HE-DLinear with soft learnable regime gating ($b_g=-3.0$), validation-guarded spatial fallback, and $\Pi_{\mathcal{C}}$ achieves \textbf{\ReviewerArrivalWAPE{}\% arrivals WAPE} and \textbf{\ReviewerNightsWAPE{}\% nights WAPE}---outperforming Vanilla DLinear (\ReviewerDLinearArrivalWAPE{}\% / \ReviewerDLinearNightsWAPE{}\%) on both targets while eliminating 100\% of physical accounting violations (0.000\% violation rate). Under crisis stress tests, the regime-aware framework substantially reduces shock errors in both the 2020--2021 Eurostat pandemic fold (66.69\% versus 76.98\% WAPE for DLinear) and the 2020 World Bank global shock (13.14\% versus 43.78\%), while supplying calibrated conformal prediction intervals.
\end{abstract}

\end{IEEEkeywords}}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{I}{n} tourism economics and hospitality operations, accurately forecasting international tourist arrivals ($Y_{\text{arr}}$) and overnight stays ($Y_{\text{nig}}$) provides essential empirical evidence for destination carrying capacity planning, infrastructure allocation, and public governance \cite{song2019tourism}. Multi-horizon projections across operational horizons $H \in \{3, 6, 12\}$ months assist tourism authorities in managing seasonal fluctuations and mitigating capacity strain during peak travel seasons \cite{clements1998forecasting}.

Tourism demand forecasting has evolved from classical econometric time-series methods \cite{athanasopoulos2011tourism} and machine-learning combinations \cite{claveria2016tourism} to deep recurrent networks \cite{hochreiter1997lstm,law2019tourism} and gradient-boosted decision trees such as LightGBM \cite{ke2017lightgbm} and CatBoost \cite{dorogush2018catboost}. In long-term time-series forecasting (LTSF), complex Transformer architectures---including Informer \cite{zhou2021informer}, Autoformer \cite{wu2021autoformer}, PatchTST \cite{nie2023patchtst}, TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}, and iTransformer \cite{liu2024itransformer}---have been widely explored. However, Zeng et al. \cite{zeng2023dlinear} demonstrated that a simple decomposition-linear architecture (\textbf{DLinear}), which uses moving-average pooling to separate trend and seasonal components before projecting them linearly, matches or outperforms complex Transformers with minimal parameter overhead.

Nevertheless, standard DLinear was not designed for multi-country macroeconomic panels, where three domain challenges persist:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Market Regime Transitions:} Structural breaks such as the COVID-19 pandemic collapse and subsequent recovery rebounds \cite{gossling2020pandemics,hale2021oxford} fundamentally alter time-series trajectories. Static linear weights cannot adapt dynamically to unobserved phase transitions.
    \item \textbf{Exogenous and Spatial Dependencies:} Deterministic calendar schedules (holidays, bridge days), macroeconomic price shocks (HICP), and cross-border regional spillovers (e.g., Schengen mobility) exert strong predictive influences that endogenous univariate models ignore.
    \item \textbf{Domain Accounting Inconsistencies:} Independent projections of arrivals and overnight stays frequently produce physically absurd forecasts where $\text{Nights} < \text{Arrivals}$ \cite{unwto2010recommendations}, violating fundamental tourism accounting definitions while lacking finite-sample calibrated uncertainty intervals.
\end{enumerate}

To address these limitations while preserving the parsimony of linear decomposition, we propose \textbf{RA-HE-DLinear} (\textit{Regime-Aware Hierarchical-Economic DLinear}). As illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:architecture}, our primary contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Regime-Aware Temporal Engine (RA-Exo-DLinear):} We formulate an extended Generalized Additive Model (GAM) backbone where trend projections are modulated by causal Hidden Markov Model (HMM) regime embeddings via Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM) equipped with a soft learnable gate ($b_g=-3.0$), coupled with dual linear projections of future calendar schedules and past macroeconomic/epidemiological signals.
    \item \textbf{Graph-Free Spatial Factorization (TRMF):} We incorporate low-rank Temporal Regularized Matrix Factorization to capture cross-country demand co-movements without imposing ad-hoc geographic distance matrices, combined with a validation-guarded gating mechanism that prevents spatial drift during heterogeneous recoveries.
    \item \textbf{Physical Consistency Projection ($\Pi_{\mathcal{C}}$):} We introduce an arrivals-anchored closed-form projection operator coupled with a scale-normalized training penalty that strictly enforces $\hat{Y}^{\text{nig}} \ge \hat{Y}^{\text{arr}} \times \text{LOS}_{\min}$, eliminating 100\% of physical accounting violations without degrading count accuracy.
    \item \textbf{Conformal Uncertainty Quantification:} We construct nominal 90\% prediction intervals via post-projection held-out residual calibration, evaluating empirical coverage and temporal exchangeability limits.
    \item \textbf{Rigorous Benchmarking and Factorial Diagnostics:} We conduct matched Eurostat and World Bank experiments, a three-seed $2^3$ factorial ablation, out-of-training crisis stress tests, income-stratified evaluations, and direct length-of-stay (LOS) error decompositions, alongside a formal point-in-time (PIT) data availability audit.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\noindent \textbf{Tourism Demand Forecasting:} Classical econometric models such as SARIMA, ETS, and error-correction models remain foundational benchmarks in tourism forecasting competitions \cite{song2019tourism,athanasopoulos2011tourism,clements1998forecasting}. Claveria et al. \cite{claveria2016tourism} evaluated machine-learning forecast combinations for regional tourism, while Law et al. \cite{law2019tourism} introduced attention-based recurrent networks \cite{hochreiter1997lstm} for monthly arrival forecasting. Tree-based ensembles including LightGBM \cite{ke2017lightgbm} and CatBoost \cite{dorogush2018catboost} offer strong tabular baselines when exogenous covariates are present.

\noindent \textbf{Linear LTSF Architectures and Regime Switching:} While Transformer variants \cite{zhou2021informer,wu2021autoformer,nie2023patchtst,wu2023timesnet,liu2024itransformer} model complex temporal dependencies, Zeng et al. \cite{zeng2023dlinear} showed that linear moving-average decomposition (DLinear, NLinear) achieves superior stability on seasonal sequences. However, unconditioned linear layers are blind to macroeconomic phase shifts. Hamilton's Markov-switching framework \cite{hamilton1989regime} formalizes latent economic regimes, and Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM) \cite{perez2018film} enables lightweight affine conditioning of intermediate representations. Conditioning only the trend branch on forward-filtered HMM states allows regime adaptation without over-parameterizing the seasonal cycle.

\noindent \textbf{Spatial Spillovers, Coherence, and Uncertainty:} Temporal Regularized Matrix Factorization (TRMF) models high-dimensional correlated series via autoregressive low-rank latent factors \cite{yu2016trmf}, which can be combined with temporal experts via mixture-of-experts gating \cite{jacobs1991adaptive,shazeer2017moe}. While Kourentzes and Athanasopoulos \cite{kourentzes2019coherent} reconcile hierarchical tourism forecasts across geographic and temporal aggregations, joint forecasting of arrivals and overnight stays requires enforcing a cross-target inequality floor ($\text{Nights} \ge \text{Arrivals}$) \cite{unwto2010recommendations}. Finally, split conformal prediction provides distribution-free coverage guarantees under exchangeability \cite{vovk2005algorithmic,angelopoulos2021conformal}.

\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Problem Formulation}
Consider a multi-destination tourism panel of $N$ entities observed across reference periods $t = 1, \dots, T$. For entity $i$ at time $t$, the target vector $\mathbf{y}_{i, t} = [Y_{i, t}^{\text{arr}}, Y_{i, t}^{\text{nig}}]^T \in \mathbb{R}_+^2$ denotes tourist arrivals and overnight stays. Given a lookback window $L$ and forecast horizon $H$, the objective is to predict $\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{i, t+1:t+H} \in \mathbb{R}_+^{H \times 2}$ from endogenous history $\mathbf{Y}_{i, t-L+1:t} \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times 2}$, past macroeconomic and epidemiological indicators $\mathbf{X}_{i, t-L+1:t}^{\text{past}} \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times D_{\text{past}}}$, deterministic future calendar schedules $\mathbf{X}_{i, t+1:t+H}^{\text{fut}} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times D_{\text{fut}}}$, and causal regime belief $\boldsymbol{\pi}_t$, subject to the physical domain accounting floor:
\begin{equation}
\hat{Y}_{i, \tau}^{\text{nig}} \ge \hat{Y}_{i, \tau}^{\text{arr}} \times \text{LOS}_{\min}, \quad \forall \tau \in \{t+1, \dots, t+H\},
\label{eq:physical_floor}
\end{equation}
where $\text{LOS}_{\min} = 1.0$ nights. Under UN Tourism (UNWTO) and Eurostat IRTS 2008 statistical standards \cite{unwto2010recommendations}, registered accommodation arrivals exclude same-day excursionists, requiring at least one overnight stay per arrival.

\subsection{Regime-Aware Temporal Engine (RA-Exo-DLinear)}
\label{sec:regime}
\noindent \textbf{Causal Market Regime Navigation (HMM):} To detect macroeconomic phase transitions without look-ahead leakage, we fit a $K=3$ Gaussian Hidden Markov Model on training-partition aggregate signals $\mathbf{u}_t \in \mathbb{R}^3$ comprising year-over-year arrival growth, Oxford COVID-19 stringency \cite{hale2021oxford}, and HICP accommodation price inflation \cite{eurostat_hicp_release}. Signal standardization parameters and transition matrix $\mathbf{A} \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times K}$ are estimated strictly within the training split and frozen prior to validation and test evaluation. Unlike two-sided Baum--Welch smoothing $P(S_t = k \mid \mathbf{u}_{1:T})$ which leaks future emissions, inference at forecast origin $o$ computes causal forward-filtered beliefs $\alpha_t^{(o)}(k) = P(S_t = k \mid \mathbf{u}_{1:t}^{(o)})$ recursively:
\begin{equation}
\alpha_t^{(o)}(k) \propto p\big(\mathbf{u}_t^{(o)} \mid S_t=k\big) \sum_{j=0}^{K-1}\alpha_{t-1}^{(o)}(j)A_{j,k},
\label{eq:hmm_forward}
\end{equation}
normalized to sum to unity. To ensure deterministic state semantics across folds, latent states are ordered by their fitted training arrival-growth centroids (State 1: low/crisis growth; State 0: middle/normal growth; State 2: high/recovery rebound). The most probable filtered state at the forecast origin indexes a learnable regime embedding table $\mathbf{E} \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times d_r}$ ($d_r = 8$):
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{e}_r = \mathbf{E}_{\hat{s}_o, :} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_r}, \qquad
\hat{s}_o = \operatorname*{arg\,max}_{k \in \{0,\dots,K-1\}} \alpha_o^{(o)}(k).
\label{eq:regime_embedding}
\end{equation}

\noindent \textbf{Series Decomposition and Backbone Initialization:} Following Zeng et al. \cite{zeng2023dlinear}, each endogenous target channel $k \in \{\text{arr}, \text{nig}\}$ of $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times 2}$ is padded at boundaries via endpoint replication and decomposed via a centered moving-average filter of odd kernel size $K_{\text{ma}}$ ($K_{\text{ma}}=13$ for monthly panels):
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{X}_{\text{trend}} = \operatorname{AvgPool}(\mathbf{X}, K_{\text{ma}}), \quad \mathbf{X}_{\text{seasonal}} = \mathbf{X} - \mathbf{X}_{\text{trend}}.
\label{eq:decomp}
\end{equation}
The linear temporal projections $\mathbf{W}_T, \mathbf{W}_S \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times L}$ map lookback $L$ to horizon $H$. To prevent overfitting on macroeconomic panels with limited chronological length, $\mathbf{W}_T$ is initialized to last-step persistence ($W_{T, h, L} = 1$) and $\mathbf{W}_S$ to seasonal-lag replication ($W_{S, h, L-12+h} = 1$, zero elsewhere). In our primary benchmark protocol, $\mathbf{W}_T$ and $\mathbf{W}_S$ are kept frozen at this seasonal-persistence initialization so that gradient optimization isolates the regime-conditioning and exogenous modulation heads, guaranteeing zero cross-seed variance when modulation switches are disabled.

\noindent \textbf{Soft-Gated FiLM Modulation and Dual Exogenous Streams:} While the seasonal cycle retains stationary projection $\mathbf{z}_{S,k} = \mathbf{W}_S \mathbf{X}_{\text{seasonal}, k} \in \mathbb{R}^H$, the trend projection $\mathbf{z}_{T,k} = \mathbf{W}_T \mathbf{X}_{\text{trend}, k} \in \mathbb{R}^H$ is conditioned on $\mathbf{e}_r$ via FiLM \cite{perez2018film} regulated by a channel-specific soft learnable gate $g_{r,k} = \sigma(\mathbf{w}_{g,k}^T \mathbf{e}_r + b_{g,k}) \in (0, 1)$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
f_{\text{trend}, k} &= \big(1 + g_{r,k} \tilde{\gamma}_k(\mathbf{e}_r)\big) \mathbf{z}_{T,k} + g_{r,k} \beta_k(\mathbf{e}_r)\mathbf{1}_H, \\
\tilde{\gamma}_k(\mathbf{e}_r) &= \alpha_{\text{trend}, k} \tanh(\mathbf{w}_{\gamma, k}^T \mathbf{e}_r + b_{\gamma, k}), \\
\beta_k(\mathbf{e}_r) &= \mathbf{w}_{\beta, k}^T \mathbf{e}_r + b_{\beta, k}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:film_trend}
\end{equation}
Initializing gate weights $\mathbf{w}_{g,k} = \mathbf{0}$ and intercept $b_{g,k} = -3.0$ yields $g_{r,k} \approx 0.047$ at initialization. During tranquil market regimes, the trend branch smoothly collapses to unmodulated linear projection $\mathbf{z}_{T,k}$, suppressing multiplicative FiLM parameter variance, while opening dynamically when structural breaks occur.

Dual standardized exogenous streams $(\mathbf{X}^{\text{fut}} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times D_{\text{fut}}}, \mathbf{X}^{\text{past}} \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times D_{\text{past}}})$ are projected into bounded relative deviations $\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\text{fut}, k}, \boldsymbol{\delta}_{\text{past}, k} \in \mathbb{R}^H$ for each target $k$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\delta_{\text{fut}, k, h} &= \alpha_{\text{fut}, k} \tanh(\mathbf{w}_{F, k}^T \mathbf{x}^{\text{fut}}_h + b_{F, k}), \; h = 1, \dots, H, \\
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\text{past}, k} &= \alpha_{\text{past}, k} \tanh\big(\mathbf{W}_{P, k} \operatorname{vec}(\mathbf{X}^{\text{past}}) + \mathbf{b}_{P, k}\big),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:exo_streams}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{w}_{F, k} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\text{fut}}}$ projects known future calendar features step-wise at each horizon $h$, and $\mathbf{W}_{P, k} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times (L \cdot D_{\text{past}})}$ maps the flattened lookback window of past macroeconomic indicators across all $H$ steps. Scaling coefficients $(\alpha_{\text{trend}, k}, \alpha_{\text{fut}, k}, \alpha_{\text{past}, k}) \in \mathbb{R}^3$ are trainable parameters initialized at $(0.35, 0.25, 0.20)$, with linear weights initialized to zero so training starts from the neutral seasonal-persistence baseline.

\noindent \textbf{Entity-Month Baseline Scaling and GAM Synthesis:} Because tourism volumes span several orders of magnitude across destinations and accommodation categories, exogenous deviations are scaled by an entity-specific historical baseline vector $\mathbf{S}_{\text{base}, k} \in \mathbb{R}_+^H$ computed strictly on the training partition. For monthly series with seasonal cycle $m(h) \in \{1,\dots,12\}$ at horizon $h$, $S_{\text{base}, k}(i, h) = \max\big(1.0, \operatorname{median}_{t \in \mathcal{T}_{\text{train}}, \text{month}(t)=m(h)} Y_{k, i, t}\big)$ (falling back to the entity training median $\max(1.0, \operatorname{median}_{t \in \mathcal{T}_{\text{train}}} Y_{k, i, t})$ for annual panels). Combining all branches yields an interpretable Generalized Additive Model (GAM) \cite{hastie1990gam}:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{\text{temporal}, k} = \operatorname{ReLU}\Big(& \underbrace{f_{\text{trend}, k}(\mathbf{X}_{\text{trend}, k}, \mathbf{e}_r)}_{\text{Regime-modulated trend}} + \underbrace{\mathbf{W}_S \mathbf{X}_{\text{seasonal}, k}}_{\text{Seasonal cycle}} \\
&+ \underbrace{\mathbf{S}_{\text{base}, k} \odot \boldsymbol{\delta}_{\text{fut}, k}}_{\text{Future calendar effect}} + \underbrace{\mathbf{S}_{\text{base}, k} \odot \boldsymbol{\delta}_{\text{past}, k}}_{\text{Past macro shocks}}\Big).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:temporal_forecast}
\end{equation}
Algebraically, setting $\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\text{trend}, k} = (\mathbf{B}_k - \mathbf{S}_{\text{base}, k}) \oslash \mathbf{S}_{\text{base}, k}$ where $\mathbf{B}_k = f_{\text{trend}, k} + \mathbf{W}_S \mathbf{X}_{\text{seasonal}, k}$ makes Eq.~\eqref{eq:temporal_forecast} identical to the multiplicative factor form $\operatorname{ReLU}\big(\mathbf{S}_{\text{base}, k} \odot (\mathbf{1}_H + \boldsymbol{\delta}_{\text{trend}, k} + \boldsymbol{\delta}_{\text{fut}, k} + \boldsymbol{\delta}_{\text{past}, k})\big)$, while evaluating Eq.~\eqref{eq:temporal_forecast} additively avoids numerical cancellation. Disabling FiLM and exogenous streams recovers nonnegative DLinear exactly.

\subsection{Cross-Country Dependency Modeling (TRMF) and Adaptive Fusion}
\label{sec:trmf}
For each chronological evaluation fold $b$, we construct country-level training matrices $\mathbf{Y}_k^{(b)} \in \mathbb{R}_+^{N \times T_b}$ with entries $y_{k, i, \text{total}, t}$ ($i=1,\dots,N$; $t \in \mathcal{T}_b$) for $k \in \{\text{arr}, \text{nig}\}$ using aggregate accommodation series (\texttt{accommodation\_type=total}). Across the four Eurostat folds (tranquil, disruption, recovery, and recent), input matrices have dimensions $38\times72$, $38\times96$, $38\times120$, and $38\times144$, respectively, while the annual World Bank panel has dimension $203\times21$. After forward-filling internal training gaps with entity training-median fallbacks, each row is standardized using training-only mean $\mu_{k,i}$ and standard deviation $\sigma_{k,i}$:
\begin{equation}
\widetilde{Y}_{k,i,t} = \frac{Y_{k,i,t}-\mu_{k,i}}{\sigma_{k,i}+10^{-5}}, \qquad \widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_k \approx \mathbf{W}_k\mathbf{X}_k^T,
\label{eq:trmf_scaling}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{W}_k \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times R}$ ($R=4$) represents spatial destination loadings and $\mathbf{X}_k \in \mathbb{R}^{T_b \times R}$ represents latent temporal factors \cite{yu2016trmf}:
\begin{equation}
\min_{\mathbf{W}_k, \mathbf{X}_k, \boldsymbol{\Theta}_k} \|\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_k - \mathbf{W}_k \mathbf{X}_k^T\|_F^2 + \lambda_w \|\mathbf{W}_k\|_F^2 + \lambda_x \mathcal{R}_{\text{AR}}(\mathbf{X}_k, \boldsymbol{\Theta}_k),
\label{eq:trmf_objective}
\end{equation}
with autoregressive lag set $\mathcal{L} = \{1, 2, 12\}$ for monthly panels and $\mathcal{L} = \{1, 2\}$ for annual panels. Latent factors are extrapolated recursively via $\mathbf{x}_{k, t+h} = \sum_{l \in \mathcal{L}} \boldsymbol{\theta}_{k, l} \odot \mathbf{x}_{k, t+h-l}$, rescaled to country counts $\hat{Y}^{\text{country}}_{k,i,t+h} = \mu_{k,i} + (\sigma_{k,i}+10^{-5})\mathbf{w}_{k,i}^T\mathbf{x}_{k,t+h}$, and mapped to accommodation category $c$ via training-only category shares $s_{k,i,c}$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
s_{k,i,c} &= \frac{\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_b}y_{k,i,c,t}}{\max\!\left(1,\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_b}y_{k,i,\mathrm{total},t}\right)}, \\
\hat{y}^{\mathrm{spatial}}_{k,i,c,t+h} &= s_{k,i,c}\hat{Y}^{\mathrm{country}}_{k,i,t+h}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:trmf_category_mapping}
\end{equation}

\noindent \textbf{Spatial--Temporal Mixture-of-Experts and Protective Fallback:} Temporal and spatial predictions are blended convexly \cite{jacobs1991adaptive,shazeer2017moe}:
\begin{equation}
\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{\text{fused}} = w_{\text{time}} \hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{\text{temporal}} + (1 - w_{\text{time}}) \hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{\text{spatial}}.
\label{eq:convex_blend}
\end{equation}
In the \textit{Static Spatial Blend} configuration, $w_{\text{time}}^* = \sigma(\theta^*)$ is tuned on held-out validation data $\mathcal{D}_{\text{val}}$ via regularized Brent optimization:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\theta^* &= \operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\theta \in \mathbb{R}} \left\{ \mathcal{L}_{\text{val}}\big(\sigma(\theta)\big) + \lambda \theta^2 \right\}, \\
w_{\text{time}}^* &= \sigma(\theta^*) = (1 + e^{-\theta^*})^{-1},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:static_gate}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_{\text{val}}$ is the mean of arrivals and nights validation WAPE and $\lambda = 0.005$ shrinks the logit toward equal weighting, yielding $w_{\text{time}}^* \approx 0.826$ in the recent Eurostat fold. However, when open-loop TRMF factors drift over multi-year post-crisis horizons, forcing $1 - w_{\text{time}}^* \approx 0.174$ weight onto degraded spatial forecasts can dilute a stronger temporal core. Therefore, the \textit{Adaptive Gated} configuration enforces a validation-guarded fallback rule over $w \in [0, 1]$ with $\hat{w} = \operatorname*{arg\,min}_{w \in [0, 1]} \mathcal{L}_{\text{val}}(w)$:
\begin{equation}
w_{\text{time}}^{\text{adapt}} = \begin{cases}
\hat{w}, & \text{if } \mathcal{L}_{\text{val}}(\hat{w}) < \mathcal{L}_{\text{val}}(1.0) - \epsilon, \\
1.0, & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\label{eq:adaptive_fallback}
\end{equation}
where $\epsilon = 10^{-5}$, defaulting to the pure temporal core ($w_{\text{time}}^{\text{adapt}} = 1.0$) whenever spatial blending does not strictly reduce validation WAPE.

\subsection{Economic Constraint Projection \texorpdfstring{($\Pi_{\mathcal{C}}$)}{(Pi\_C)}}
We define the feasible accounting cone $\mathcal{C} = \{(u, v) \in \mathbb{R}_+^2 \mid v \ge u \times 1.0\}$ \cite{unwto2010recommendations}. During neural training, a scale-normalized coupled consistency loss penalizes unprojected violations on raw outputs so gradients guide representations toward $\mathcal{C}$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} &= \sum_{k \in \{\text{arr}, \text{nig}\}} \left\|\frac{\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_k - \mathbf{Y}_k}{\mathbf{S}_{\text{base}, k}}\right\|_2^2 \\
&\quad + \lambda_{\text{cons}} \left\|\frac{\operatorname{ReLU}(\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{\text{arr}} - \hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{\text{nig}})}{\mathbf{S}_{\text{base}, \text{arr}}}\right\|_2^2,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:coupled_loss}
\end{equation}
where division is elementwise and $\lambda_{\text{cons}} = 0.2$. At inference, after spatial--temporal fusion, an arrivals-anchored closed-form projection operator $\Pi_{\mathcal{C}}$ strictly guarantees physical feasibility:
\begin{equation}
\Pi_{\mathcal{C}}(\hat{Y}_{\text{arr}}, \hat{Y}_{\text{nig}}) = \left(\hat{Y}_{\text{arr}}, \, \max\left(\hat{Y}_{\text{nig}}, \, \hat{Y}_{\text{arr}} \times 1.0\right)\right).
\label{eq:projection}
\end{equation}
Because accommodation arrivals have higher signal-to-noise ratio and lower forecast error than overnight stays, anchoring on $\hat{Y}_{\text{arr}}$ leaves arrivals accuracy invariant while rectifying violating nights predictions.

\subsection{Uncertainty Quantification (Conformal DCQF)}
\label{sec:conformal_method}
Split conformal prediction supplies finite-sample marginal coverage under exchangeability \cite{vovk2005algorithmic,angelopoulos2021conformal}. Calibration is performed \textbf{post-projection} on held-out 2023 observed outcome pairs $\mathcal{D}_{\text{cal}}$ (excluding imputed entries). Nonconformity scores use projected point predictions $\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_i^* = \Pi_{\mathcal{C}}(\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_i^{\text{fused}})$:
\begin{equation}
s_{i, k} = \big|y_{i, k} - \hat{Y}_{i, k}^*\big|, \qquad k \in \{\text{arr}, \text{nig}\}.
\end{equation}
With $\hat{Q}_{1-\alpha, k}$ denoting the empirical quantile at level $\lceil (N_{\text{cal}} + 1)(1 - \alpha) \rceil / N_{\text{cal}}$ ($\alpha = 0.10$), the nominal 90\% prediction interval is:
\begin{equation}
\hat{\mathcal{I}}_{1-\alpha, k} = \left[\max\left(0, \hat{Y}_k^* - \hat{Q}_{1-\alpha, k}\right), \, \hat{Y}_k^* + \hat{Q}_{1-\alpha, k}\right].
\label{eq:conformal_interval}
\end{equation}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:pipeline} summarizes the end-to-end training, validation gating, projection, and conformal calibration workflow.

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\caption{RA-HE-DLinear Training and Inference Pipeline}
\label{alg:pipeline}
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Panel histories $\mathbf{Y}$, exogenous streams $(\mathbf{X}^{\text{fut}}, \mathbf{X}^{\text{past}})$, chronological partitions $\{\mathcal{D}_{\text{train}}, \mathcal{D}_{\text{val}}, \mathcal{D}_{\text{cal}}, \mathcal{D}_{\text{test}}\}$.
\STATE Fit 3-state Gaussian HMM on $\mathcal{D}_{\text{train}}$ aggregate signals; order states by training arrival growth; compute forward-filtered origin states $\hat{s}_o$ via Eq.~\eqref{eq:hmm_forward}.
\STATE Fit dual TRMF factors $(\mathbf{W}_k, \mathbf{X}_k, \boldsymbol{\Theta}_k)$ and category shares $s_{k,i,c}$ on $\mathcal{D}_{\text{train}}$ via Eqs.~\eqref{eq:trmf_scaling}--\eqref{eq:trmf_category_mapping}.
\STATE Initialize $\mathbf{W}_T, \mathbf{W}_S$ to persistence/seasonal lags; compute training medians $\mathbf{S}_{\text{base}, k}$.
\FOR{each epoch on $\mathcal{D}_{\text{train}}$}
    \STATE Decompose series into $\mathbf{X}_{\text{trend}}$ and $\mathbf{X}_{\text{seasonal}}$ via kernel $K_{\text{ma}}$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:decomp}.
    \STATE Modulate trend via soft-gated FiLM($\mathbf{e}_r$) in Eq.~\eqref{eq:film_trend}; project exogenous streams via Eq.~\eqref{eq:exo_streams}; compute $\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{\text{temporal}}$ via Eq.~\eqref{eq:temporal_forecast}.

\ENDFOR
\STATE Select spatial--temporal weight $w_{\text{time}}$ on $\mathcal{D}_{\text{val}}$ via Eq.~\eqref{eq:static_gate} or adaptive guard Eq.~\eqref{eq:adaptive_fallback}.
\STATE Compute projected calibration forecasts on $\mathcal{D}_{\text{cal}}$ and empirical 90\% quantiles $\hat{Q}_{1-\alpha, k}$.

\subsection{Reference-Period Protocol and Point-in-Time Considerations}
\label{sec:availability}
The experiments reported in this paper follow a \textit{retrospective reference-period protocol}: observations are indexed by reference month or year, and HMM parameters, scalers, and TRMF loadings are fitted strictly within the training partition with causal forward-filtered origin states $\hat{s}_o$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:hmm_forward}. In real-time operational deployment, however, statistical agencies publish data with reporting lags and subsequent revisions. Specifically, Eurostat releases country-level monthly accommodation statistics approximately 45 days to 2.5 months after the reference month \cite{eurostat_tourism_release}, HICP breakdowns around mid-month following the reference period \cite{eurostat_hicp_release}, and World Bank WDI indicators with annual revisions \cite{worldbank_revisions,worldbank_archives}.

For a strict point-in-time (PIT) operational backtest at issuance timestamp $o$, let $r_{i,j,s,v}$ denote the release timestamp of vintage $v$ for variable $j$, entity $i$, and reference period $s$. Admissible inputs must satisfy the vintage selection rule:
\begin{equation}
v^*(i,j,s;o) = \operatorname*{arg\,max}_{v:\,r_{i,j,s,v}\le o}r_{i,j,s,v}, \qquad
x_{i,j,s}^{(o)} = x_{i,j,s,v^*(i,j,s;o)}.
\label{eq:asof_selection}
\end{equation}
When $r_{i,j,s,v} > o$ for all $v$, period $s$ remains unreleased at $o$, requiring HMM beliefs in Eq.~\eqref{eq:hmm_forward} to propagate through transition matrix $\mathbf{A}$ without emission updates. Because historical release-vintage ledgers were not retained in the upstream snapshots, our empirical tables evaluate reference-period accuracy while logging exact snapshot hashes and origin timestamps as a reproducible baseline.

\label{sec:matched_benchmarks}

\subsection{Length of Stay and Joint Target Diagnostics}
\label{sec:los_analysis}
For matching destination, category, and month, length of stay is defined as $\operatorname{LOS}_{i,t} = Y^{\text{nig}}_{i,t} / Y^{\text{arr}}_{i,t}$ and $\widehat{\operatorname{LOS}}_{i,t} = \hat{Y}^{\text{nig}}_{i,t} / \hat{Y}^{\text{arr}}_{i,t}$. Writing count errors as $\epsilon_A = \hat{Y}^{\text{arr}} - Y^{\text{arr}}$ and $\epsilon_N = \hat{Y}^{\text{nig}} - Y^{\text{nig}}$, the exact ratio-error identity for $\hat{Y}^{\text{arr}} > 0$ is:
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\operatorname{LOS}} - \operatorname{LOS} = \frac{\epsilon_N - \operatorname{LOS}\,\epsilon_A}{\hat{Y}^{\mathrm{arr}}}.
\label{eq:los_error_decomposition}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:los_error_decomposition} demonstrates two critical properties: proportional count errors ($\epsilon_N \approx \operatorname{LOS}\,\epsilon_A$) cancel in the ratio, whereas near-zero predicted arrivals ($\hat{Y}^{\text{arr}} \to 0$) cause ratio errors to explode. Table~\ref{tab:reviewer_los} confirms this empirically across all four chronological folds on aggregate accommodation series (\texttt{total}). While DLinear produces undefined zero-arrival ratios in 10 pre-pandemic, 82 pandemic, 2 recovery, and 7 recent country-months, \textbf{RA-HE (Static)} produces \textbf{zero undefined ratios} across all folds because positive TRMF spatial factors ($1 - w_{\text{time}}^* = 0.174$ in the recent fold) lift winter arrival denominators above zero. In the 2020--2021 pandemic fold, DLinear's LOS RMSE explodes to $9.394$ nights/arrival (driven by near-zero arrival predictions such as Iceland in March 2021), whereas RA-HE (Static) reduces LOS MAE to $0.568$ (vs.\ $0.999$) and LOS RMSE to $1.253$. In the recent fold (1,122 shared country-months), RA-HE (Static) achieves lower LOS MAE ($0.155$ vs.\ $0.182$, identical before and after $\Pi_{\mathcal{C}}$ because all 52 static-blend violations occur in disaggregated \texttt{camping} and \texttt{short\_stay} categories) and lower LOS RMSE ($0.350$ vs.\ $0.435$) than DLinear. Meanwhile, for \textbf{RA-HE (Adaptive)} ($w_{\text{time}}^{\text{adapt}} = 1.0$), applying $\Pi_{\mathcal{C}}$ rectifies 22 aggregate-series violations, lowering recent-fold LOS MAE from $0.184$ to $0.167$ and achieving the lowest overall LOS RMSE of \textbf{$0.342$} nights/arrival.

\section{Discussion and Management Implications}
\label{sec:management}

\noindent \textbf{Decision-Making Across Market Regimes:} Our multi-fold evaluation highlights a fundamental trade-off in tourism forecasting. During tranquil, highly seasonal periods (such as 2024--2026 in Europe), minimal-parameter Seasonal Naive (\ReviewerSNaiveArrivalWAPE{}\% / \ReviewerSNaiveNightsWAPE{}\% WAPE) and Vanilla DLinear (\ReviewerDLinearArrivalWAPE{}\% / \ReviewerDLinearNightsWAPE{}\% WAPE) provide strong point-count baselines. However, relying solely on unconditioned linear or naive baselines exposes destination management organizations (DMOs) to two operational risks: (1) catastrophic forecast breakdown during structural shocks (where DLinear reaches 76.98\% WAPE in the 2020--2021 Eurostat fold and 54.93\% on the World Bank panel), and (2) physically inconsistent projections where $\hat{Y}^{\text{nig}} < \hat{Y}^{\text{arr}}$ or undefined zero-arrival LOS ratios (82 undefined cases for DLinear in 2020--2021). By combining exogenous calendar/macro projections with soft regime gating ($b_g=-3.0$), validation-guarded spatial fusion, and $\Pi_{\mathcal{C}}$, \textbf{RA-HE-DLinear (Adaptive Gated)} improves upon Vanilla DLinear even in the tranquil recent fold (\ReviewerArrivalWAPE{}\% / \ReviewerNightsWAPE{}\% WAPE) while guaranteeing 0.000\% physical violations and robust shock protection.

\noindent \textbf{Country Case Studies and LOS Planning:} Figures~\ref{fig:tourism_cases}--\ref{fig:los_cases} examine three representative destinations selected at the median of each pre-test 2023/2019 recovery tercile: Cyprus (recovery ratio 0.98; arrivals WAPE 13.31\% RA-HE Adaptive vs.\ 17.05\% DLinear and 18.57\% RA-HE Static; Static LOS MAE 0.295 nights/arrival), Sweden (ratio 1.03; arrivals WAPE 6.13\% RA-HE Adaptive vs.\ 8.00\% DLinear and 10.47\% RA-HE Static; Static LOS MAE 0.043), and Denmark (ratio 1.14; arrivals WAPE 7.63\% RA-HE Adaptive vs.\ 9.38\% DLinear and 12.34\% RA-HE Static; Static LOS MAE 0.158). For hospitality capacity planning, arrival forecasts govern check-in and border-processing throughput, whereas overnight-stay forecasts govern room-night demand and seasonal staffing. Tracking both count accuracy and derived LOS ratios ($\hat{Y}^{\text{nig}}/\hat{Y}^{\text{arr}}$) within nominal 90\% conformal bands enables DMOs to stress-test infrastructure requirements under both baseline seasonal cycles and downside macroeconomic shocks.

\noindent \textbf{Limitations:} First, the Eurostat benchmark evaluates annual January forecast origins over a 12-month horizon; higher-frequency rolling origins would better capture mid-year policy shifts. Second, in the recent 2024--2026 fold, the HMM remained in a single stationary regime across origins, so multi-seed dispersion reflects training initialization variability rather than regime-transition variance. Third, non-OECD overnight stays in the World Bank panel are semi-synthetic ALOS imputations rather than primary survey counts. Finally, empirically quantifying forecast degradation from reporting delays and data revisions requires constructing a timestamped point-in-time vintage ledger following Eq.~\eqref{eq:asof_selection}.

\section{Conclusion}
We proposed and evaluated \textbf{RA-HE-DLinear} for multi-country tourism demand forecasting. Across \ReviewerObservedPairs{} observed test pairs in the recent Eurostat panel (January 2024--July 2026), minimal-parameter Seasonal Naive (\ReviewerSNaiveArrivalWAPE{}\% / \ReviewerSNaiveNightsWAPE{}\% WAPE) excels during stationary seasonal cycles, whereas unconstrained statistical, tree-based, and linear baselines violate physical domain accounting rules (up to 8.786\% violation rate) and fail during crisis breaks. Controlled three-seed factorials demonstrate that direct exogenous calendar and price projections consistently reduce forecast error ($-1.75\text{ pp}$ arrivals and $-2.59\text{ pp}$ nights WAPE), while soft learnable gating ($b_g=-3.0$) and validation-guarded fusion prevent FiLM variance and spatial factor drift. Equipped with these mechanisms and closed-form projection $\Pi_{\mathcal{C}}$, \textbf{RA-HE-DLinear} outperforms Vanilla DLinear in the recent Eurostat benchmark (\ReviewerArrivalWAPE{}\% vs.\ \ReviewerDLinearArrivalWAPE{}\% arrivals WAPE; \ReviewerNightsWAPE{}\% vs.\ \ReviewerDLinearNightsWAPE{}\% nights WAPE), achieves substantial error reductions during the 2020--2021 Eurostat pandemic shock (66.69\% vs.\ 76.98\%) and 2020 World Bank shock (13.14\% vs.\ 43.78\%), eliminates 100\% of physical accounting violations (0.000\%), and provides calibrated conformal prediction intervals (\ReviewerArrivalCoverage{}\% and \ReviewerNightsCoverage{}\% empirical coverage).
