\title{RA-HE-DLinear: A Regime-Aware Hierarchical-Economic Linear Framework for Multi-Country Tourism Demand Forecasting}

\author{Nguyen-Tuan~Tran,
        Hoai-Bao~Phan}

\IEEEtitleabstractindextext{%
\begin{abstract}
Forecasting tourism arrivals and overnight stays requires models that accommodate seasonality, economic disruption, and variation across destinations. We evaluate RA-HE-DLinear, which combines a decomposition-linear temporal model, HMM conditioning, exogenous inputs, low-rank spatial factors, and physical projection. The Eurostat benchmark uses 152 destination--accommodation series, January forecast origins, and \ReviewerObservedPairs{} observed target pairs. The full model records arrivals WAPE of \ReviewerArrivalWAPE{}\% and nights WAPE of \ReviewerNightsWAPE{}\%. Seasonal naive and DLinear achieve lower recent-test error for both targets. A three-seed factorial ablation with a shared validation gate finds lower fused WAPE from direct exogenous projections, smaller reductions from HMM conditioning, and higher WAPE from multiplicative FiLM gains. Separate chronological folds, income groups, and errors of the nights-to-arrivals ratio characterize differences across tourism settings. Nominal 90\% residual intervals are evaluated for coverage and width. The experiments use retrospective reference-period inputs. Historical data vintages are needed to evaluate operational accuracy at each forecast origin.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Tourism demand forecasting, DLinear, Hidden Markov Models, Temporal Regularized Matrix Factorization, economic constraints, conformal prediction.
\end{IEEEkeywords}}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{I}{n} tourism economics and hospitality operations, forecasting international tourist arrivals ($Y_{\text{arr}}$) and overnight stays ($Y_{\text{nig}}$) provides essential empirical evidence for destination carrying capacity planning, resource allocation, and governance policies \cite{song2019tourism}. Multi-horizon projections across operational horizons $H \in \{3, 6, 12\}$ months assist tourism authorities in managing seasonal fluctuations and mitigating infrastructure strain during peak demand periods.

Tourism demand forecasting uses classical time-series methods \cite{athanasopoulos2011tourism}, combinations of machine-learning forecasts \cite{claveria2016tourism}, and deep recurrent models \cite{law2019tourism}. LightGBM \cite{ke2017lightgbm} and CatBoost \cite{dorogush2018catboost} provide tree-boosting approaches for tabular inputs. In long-term time series forecasting benchmarks, Zeng et al. \cite{zeng2023dlinear} showed that DLinear can match or outperform more complex Transformer models. DLinear uses moving-average pooling to separate trend and seasonal components before projecting them.

However, standard DLinear was not designed for macroeconomic forecasting panels, where three persistent challenges arise:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Market Regime Transitions:} Structural breaks (e.g., COVID-19 pandemic shocks, recovery rebounds) fundamentally alter time-series trajectories. Static linear weights cannot adapt dynamically to unobserved phase transitions.
    \item \textbf{Exogenous and Spatial Dependencies:} Calendar schedules (bridge days, school vacations), macroeconomic price shocks (HICP), and regional travel spillovers (Schengen mobility) exert dominant predictive influences that unconditioned univariate models ignore.
    \item \textbf{Domain Accounting Inconsistencies:} Projections of arrivals and overnight stays often produce physically absurd forecasts where $\text{Nights} < \text{Arrivals}$, violating fundamental accounting definitions while lacking finite-sample calibrated uncertainty intervals.
\end{enumerate}

To overcome these challenges, this study proposes \textbf{RA-HE-DLinear} (\textit{Regime-Aware Hierarchical-Economic DLinear}). The principal contributions of this work are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Regime-Aware Temporal Engine (RA-Exo-DLinear):} Formulating a Generalized Additive Model (GAM) architecture where trend representations are conditioned on Hidden Markov Model (HMM) regimes via Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM), integrated with projections of future calendar schedules and past macroeconomic signals. Operational use requires the release-aware regime estimation specified below.
    \item \textbf{Graph-Free Spatial Factorization (TRMF):} Incorporating low-rank Temporal Regularized Matrix Factorization to capture cross-country tourism spillovers across destinations without imposing ad-hoc geographic distance matrices.
    \item \textbf{Physical Consistency Projection.} An arrivals-anchored operator enforces the specified lower bound on predicted nights. The experiments measure its intervention rate and its effect on count errors and the nights-to-arrivals ratio separately.
    \item \textbf{Uncertainty Quantification.} Held-out residual calibration supplies nominal 90\% intervals. Their empirical coverage and width are measured on observed test outcomes, with the temporal exchangeability assumption stated explicitly.
    \item \textbf{Tourism Benchmarking and Diagnostics.} A matched Eurostat experiment includes seasonal naive, ETS/Holt--Winters, SARIMA, SARIMAX, LightGBM, DLinear, and component configurations. Out-of-training stress folds, destination groups, direct LOS errors, and three country cases examine where each model succeeds or fails. Section~\ref{sec:availability} defines the further release-vintage restrictions needed for an operational evaluation.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\noindent \textbf{Tourism Demand Forecasting:} Classical methods such as SARIMA and error-correction models provide benchmarks for tourism demand forecasting \cite{song2019tourism,athanasopoulos2011tourism}. Claveria et al. \cite{claveria2016tourism} evaluate combinations of machine-learning forecasts for regional tourism demand. Law et al. \cite{law2019tourism} use a recurrent neural network with attention to forecast monthly arrivals in Macau.

\noindent \textbf{LTSF Linear Architectures and Market Regimes:} Zeng et al. \cite{zeng2023dlinear} challenged complex Transformers by showing that linear decomposition models (DLinear, NLinear) achieve superior efficiency on temporal sequences. Nonetheless, pure linear architectures are blind to regime transitions. Hamilton's Markov-switching models \cite{hamilton1989regime} formulated business cycle regimes, while Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM) \cite{perez2018film} enables affine transformations on intermediate representations conditioned on external context. HMM regimes can condition the trend branch without heavy parameterization. Forecasting applications additionally require state estimation from observations available at the forecast origin.

\noindent \textbf{Spatial Spillovers, Constraints, and Uncertainty:} Temporal Regularized Matrix Factorization (TRMF) represents high-dimensional time series using low-rank temporal factors \cite{yu2016trmf}. Kourentzes and Athanasopoulos \cite{kourentzes2019coherent} reconcile Australian tourism forecasts across geographic and temporal aggregations. For the two accommodation targets studied here, we apply a projection that sets a lower bound on predicted overnight stays relative to predicted arrivals. Conformal prediction provides finite-sample coverage guarantees under exchangeability \cite{vovk2005algorithmic,angelopoulos2021conformal}.

\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Problem Formulation}
Consider a tourism panel of $N$ destinations observed across time $t = 1, \dots, T$. For destination $i$ at time $t$, target vector $\mathbf{y}_{i, t} = [Y_{i, t}^{\text{arr}}, Y_{i, t}^{\text{nig}}]^T \in \mathbb{R}_+^2$ denotes tourist arrivals and overnight stays. Given a lookback window $L$ and forecast horizon $H$, the objective is to estimate $\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{i, t+1:t+H}$ from endogenous history $\mathbf{Y}_{i, t-L+1:t}$, past macroeconomic indicators $\mathbf{X}_{i, t-L+1:t}^{\text{past}}$, known future calendar schedules $\mathbf{X}_{i, t+1:t+H}^{\text{fut}}$, and latent regime belief $\boldsymbol{\pi}_t$, subject to the physical accounting floor:
\begin{equation}
\hat{Y}_{i, \tau}^{\text{nig}} \ge \hat{Y}_{i, \tau}^{\text{arr}} \times \text{LOS}_{\min}, \quad \forall \tau \in [t+1, t+H],
\label{eq:physical_floor}
\end{equation}
where $\text{LOS}_{\min} = 1.0$ nights. Under UN Tourism (UNWTO) and Eurostat IRTS 2008 standards, overnight tourists strictly require $\text{LOS} \ge 1.0$ nights (same-day excursionists are excluded from arrivals).

Here $t$ indexes a reference period. A separate timestamp $o$ identifies when a forecast is issued. An observation for period $t$ enters a point-in-time history only after its release at or before $o$. Section~\ref{sec:availability} defines this information restriction for targets, exogenous variables, and regime signals.

\subsection{Regime-Aware Temporal Engine (RA-Exo-DLinear)}
\label{sec:regime}
\noindent \textbf{Market Regime Navigation (HMM).} The original unified pipeline uses a Gaussian HMM with three states and signals comprising aggregate arrival growth, COVID stringency, and a price/inflation indicator. It fits and decodes states on the complete panel before splitting targets, and estimates normalization from the supplied sequence. Its \texttt{predict\_proba} call uses smoothed state posteriors. These operations motivate the information audit in Section~\ref{sec:availability}. The reproduced Eurostat experiments instead use the aggregate accommodation category, fit HMM parameters and signal normalization on each training fold, and compute a forward recursion over the reference-period sequence. The state at each January origin conditions the entire forecast block. This removes later reference-period emissions from the new regime feature, while historical publication delays and covariate vintages remain unresolved.

For the availability-constrained evaluation, HMM parameters, signal scalers, and state-label ordering must be estimated from the admissible training data and frozen before validation and test issuance. Let $\mathcal{F}_o$ contain releases available by $o$, and let $\mathbf{u}_t^{(o)}$ denote the signal coordinates at reference time $t$ admitted by this information set. The filtered belief is $\alpha_t^{(o)}(k)=P(S_t=k\mid\mathbf{u}_{1:t}^{(o)})$. With these parameters fixed, the forward recursion is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\alpha_t^{(o)}(k) &\propto p(\mathbf{u}_t^{(o)} \mid S_t=k) \\
&\quad\times\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}\alpha_{t-1}^{(o)}(j)A_{j,k},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
normalized to sum to one. A missing signal coordinate is marginalized out of the Gaussian emission density. If all coordinates are unavailable, the step uses the transition distribution alone. To cover the interval from the most recent signal to forecast issuance and all $H$ future periods, beliefs are propagated through $A$ without inserting realized future emissions. Training-state ordering uses training growth statistics only. Smoothed beliefs $P(S_t=k\mid\mathbf{u}_{1:T})$ are appropriate for retrospective labeling. For prediction, the specified evaluation uses the filtered and transition-propagated belief at $o$. To retain the existing FiLM interface, its maximum-probability state indexes the learned eight-dimensional embedding table $\mathbf{E}\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times8}$,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{e}_r=\mathbf{E}_{\hat{s}_o,:},\qquad
\hat{s}_o=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{k}\pi_o(k).
\end{equation}

\noindent \textbf{Series Decomposition and FiLM Modulation:} Input series $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times C}$ is decomposed into trend and seasonal branches via moving-average pooling:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{X}_{\text{trend}} = \text{AvgPool}(\mathbf{X}, K_{\text{ma}}), \quad \mathbf{X}_{\text{seasonal}} = \mathbf{X} - \mathbf{X}_{\text{trend}}.
\end{equation}
The seasonal branch is projected via stationary weights $\mathbf{W}_S \mathbf{X}_{\text{seasonal}}$. The trend branch is modulated via FiLM \cite{perez2018film} conditioned on $\mathbf{e}_r$:
\begin{equation}
f_{\text{trend}}(\mathbf{X}_{\text{trend}}, \mathbf{e}_r) = \boldsymbol{\gamma}(\mathbf{e}_r) \odot (\mathbf{W}_T \mathbf{X}_{\text{trend}}) + \boldsymbol{\beta}(\mathbf{e}_r),
\end{equation}
with bounded scaling $\boldsymbol{\gamma}(\mathbf{e}_r) = 1.0 + \alpha_{\text{trend}} \cdot \tanh(\mathbf{W}_\gamma \mathbf{e}_r + \mathbf{b}_\gamma)$ and $\boldsymbol{\beta}(\mathbf{e}_r) = \mathbf{W}_\beta \mathbf{e}_r + \mathbf{b}_\beta$. Dual exogenous streams $(\mathbf{X}_{\text{fut}}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{past}})$ are projected independently:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\text{fut}} &= \alpha_{\text{fut}} \tanh(\operatorname{Linear}(\mathbf{X}_{\text{fut}})), \\
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\text{past}} &= \alpha_{\text{past}} \tanh(\operatorname{Linear}(\mathbf{X}_{\text{past}})).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Let $B = f_{\text{trend}}(\mathbf{X}_{\text{trend}},\mathbf{e}_r) + \mathbf{W}_S\mathbf{X}_{\text{seasonal}}$ denote baseline temporal forecast, and $S_{\text{base}}$ be destination-specific historical training median (floored at 1.0). Raw point predictions instantiate a Generalized Additive Model (GAM) formulation \cite{hastie1990gam}:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\hat{Y}_{\text{raw}} = \operatorname{ReLU}\Big(& \underbrace{f_{\text{trend}}(\mathbf{X}_{\text{trend}},\mathbf{e}_r)}_{\text{Regime-modulated trend}} + \underbrace{\mathbf{W}_S\mathbf{X}_{\text{seasonal}}}_{\text{Seasonal cycle}} \\
&+ \underbrace{S_{\text{base}}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\text{fut}}}_{\text{Calendar effect}} + \underbrace{S_{\text{base}}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\text{past}}}_{\text{Macro shocks}}\Big).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:temporal_forecast}
\end{equation}
Here link function $g^{-1}(z) = \operatorname{ReLU}(z)$ enforces non-negativity. The separate algebraic terms expose model structure; their economic interpretation and incremental forecasting value require empirical tests. Setting FiLM and exogenous projections to zero recovers standard nonnegative DLinear exactly.

\subsection{Cross-Country Dependency Modeling (TRMF)}
\label{sec:trmf}
\noindent \textbf{Construction of the input matrices.} For each chronological fold $b$, we first restrict the completed training frame to \texttt{accommodation\_type=total}. We verify unique destination--month keys, sort destinations by \texttt{geo} and reference months chronologically, and pivot \texttt{arrivals\_total} and \texttt{nights\_total} separately, with \texttt{geo} as the row index and \texttt{date} as the column index. Thus
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{Y}^{(b)}_k
=\big[y_{k,i,\mathrm{total},t}\big]_{i=1,\ldots,N;\,t\in\mathcal{T}_b}
\in\mathbb{R}_+^{N\times T_b},
\label{eq:trmf_input}
\end{equation}
where $k\in\{\mathrm{arr},\mathrm{nig}\}$ identifies the target, $\mathcal{T}_b$ contains only training reference periods, and $T_b=|\mathcal{T}_b|$. For Eurostat, each cell is a monthly count of accommodation arrivals or overnight stays for one destination. A date such as \texttt{2010-01-01} labels January 2010. The two matrices use identical row and date orderings and are fitted independently. The spatial branch receives target counts; the calendar and macroeconomic variables enter the temporal branch.

The Eurostat snapshot spans 199 months, January 2010--July 2026, giving a descriptive country-total grid of $38\times199$ per target. The recent TRMF fit uses its first 144 months, January 2010--December 2021, so each actual fitting input is $38\times144$. Its rows represent 38 country-total series. The temporal branch instead uses all $38\times4=152$ destination--accommodation panel series. Table~\ref{tab:trmf_dimensions} gives the training dimensions in every fold.

\noindent \textbf{Completion and scaling.} In the reproduced Eurostat runner, each destination--category history is forward-filled before pivoting. Initial gaps use its training median, with a pooled training median and then zero as fallbacks; target counts are clipped below at zero. The resulting training matrices are finite. For each target and destination, let $\mu_{k,i}$ and $\sigma_{k,i}$ be the mean and population standard deviation over the completed training row. The factorization input is
\begin{equation}
\widetilde{Y}_{k,i,t}
=\frac{Y_{k,i,t}-\mu_{k,i}}{\sigma_{k,i}+10^{-5}},
\qquad
\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_k\approx\mathbf{W}_k\mathbf{X}_k^T.
\label{eq:trmf_scaling}
\end{equation}
Both scaling statistics are fitted within the fold's training partition. The recent matrices have $\mathbf{W}_k\in\mathbb{R}^{38\times4}$ and $\mathbf{X}_k\in\mathbb{R}^{144\times4}$; the two targets have their own factors. For the annual World Bank configuration, the training CSV contains 3,802 observed economy--year rows across 203 economies and 21 years. Pivoting gives $203\times21=4,263$ cells per target. The matrix builder fills the 461 absent cells with the corresponding economy's training-row mean. Annual dates such as \texttt{1995-01-01} label the year 1995.

\noindent \textbf{Factorization and forecast dimensions.} The idealized dual TRMF objective \cite{yu2016trmf}, written on standardized targets, is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\min_{\mathbf{W}_k, \mathbf{X}_k, \boldsymbol{\Theta}_k} \sum_{(i, t) \in \Omega} &(\widetilde{Y}_{k, i, t} - \mathbf{w}_{k, i}^T \mathbf{x}_{k, t})^2 \\
&+ \lambda_w \|\mathbf{W}_k\|_F^2 + \lambda_x \mathcal{R}_{\text{AR}}(\mathbf{X}_k, \boldsymbol{\Theta}_k)
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here $\Omega$ denotes observed entries in the idealized objective. The available implementation optimizes reconstruction on the completed matrix, including its imputed entries. Rank is four, and monthly AR lags are $\{1,2,12\}$, giving one coefficient matrix $\boldsymbol{\Theta}_k\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times4}$ per target. The annual configuration uses lags $\{1,2\}$ and coefficients of size $2\times4$. The spatial implementation alternates low-rank reconstruction updates and estimates a ridge AR model for the latent factors. Its $X$ update omits the AR penalty in the objective above, so the experiments evaluate this TRMF-inspired implementation. Reconstruction ridge coefficients are 0.01, and AR coefficients are fitted separately. Forecast factors are damped and clipped. Before these implementation bounds, the latent recursion and inverse scaling are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{x}_{k,t+h}
&=\sum_{l\in\mathcal{L}}\boldsymbol{\theta}_{k,l}\odot\mathbf{x}_{k,t+h-l},\\
\hat{Y}^{\mathrm{country}}_{k,i,t+h}
&=\mu_{k,i}+(\sigma_{k,i}+10^{-5})\mathbf{w}_{k,i}^T\mathbf{x}_{k,t+h}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:trmf_forecast}
\end{equation}
Here $\boldsymbol{\theta}_{k,l}\in\mathbb{R}^{4}$ is the lag-$l$ coefficient vector and $\odot$ is elementwise multiplication. Reconstructed country counts are clipped to $[0,\max(3\mu_{k,i},1000)]$. The recent runner extrapolates from December 2021 through July 2026, producing a $38\times55$ country-forecast matrix per target for January 2022--July 2026. Fitted spatial loadings and AR coefficients remain fixed; new tourism observations are not assimilated into this spatial forecast. Matching columns supply validation, calibration, and test predictions.

\noindent \textbf{Mapping to the 152 panel series.} For target $k$, destination $i$, and accommodation category $c$, a fixed training share maps country-total forecasts back to category-level rows:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
s_{k,i,c}
&=\frac{\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_b}y_{k,i,c,t}}
{\max\!\left(1,\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_b}y_{k,i,\mathrm{total},t}\right)},\\
\hat{y}^{\mathrm{spatial}}_{k,i,c,t+h}
&=s_{k,i,c}\hat{Y}^{\mathrm{country}}_{k,i,t+h}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:trmf_category_mapping}
\end{equation}
Arrivals and nights use separate shares estimated from completed training counts. This yields one spatial forecast for every destination--category--month key used by the temporal branch; the recent test grid contains $152\times31=4,712$ such keys per target.
Spatial and temporal predictions are blended convexly:
\begin{equation}
\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{\text{fused}} = w_{\text{time}}^* \hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{\text{temporal}} + (1 - w_{\text{time}}^*) \hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{\text{spatial}},
\end{equation}
Following the use of gating to combine expert outputs \cite{jacobs1991adaptive,shazeer2017moe}, we parameterize the blend with a single scalar logit $\theta \in \mathbb{R}$ shared across destinations, targets, and horizons within each fit.
\begin{equation}
w_{\text{time}}^* = \sigma(\theta^*) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-\theta^*}}, \quad \text{where } \theta^* = \arg\min_{\theta \in \mathbb{R}} \left\{ \mathcal{L}_{\text{val}}(\sigma(\theta)) + \lambda \theta^2 \right\}.
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal{L}_{\text{val}}$ is the mean of arrivals and nights WAPE expressed as fractions. We set $\lambda=0.005$ and minimize the objective on validation using Brent's method. The penalty shrinks the logit toward zero, which corresponds to equal weighting. The recent full-model fit selects $w_{\text{time}}^* \approx 0.826$, placing about 82.6\% of the blend on the temporal branch.

\subsection{Economic Constraint Projection \texorpdfstring{($\Pi_{\mathcal{C}}$)}{(Pi\_C)}}
Define the feasible economic cone $\mathcal{C} = \{(u, v) \in \mathbb{R}_+^2 \mid v \ge u \times 1.0\}$. All 4,683 target pairs in the enriched annual World Bank panel satisfy $Y_{\text{nig}} \ge Y_{\text{arr}}$ (minimum LOS 1.003 nights, median 6.48). The enrichment pipeline enforces this bound when preparing the annual nights target. The LightGBM violation rates are 11.20\% in the annual reference and 2.42\% in the recent Eurostat test.

The specified operator treats predicted arrivals as the anchor and adjusts nights upward when the lower bound is violated. In the Eurostat panel, both indicators refer to the same destination, accommodation category, and reference month. The asymmetric projection is
\begin{equation}
\Pi_{\mathcal{C}}(\hat{Y}_{\text{arr}}, \hat{Y}_{\text{nig}}) = \left(\hat{Y}_{\text{arr}}, \, \max\left(\hat{Y}_{\text{nig}}, \, \hat{Y}_{\text{arr}} \times 1.0\right)\right).
\label{eq:projection}
\end{equation}
During neural training, a scale-normalized coupled consistency loss penalizes unprojected violations:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{MSE}} + \lambda_{\text{cons}} \frac{1}{N_B H}\sum_{b,h}\left(\frac{\operatorname{ReLU}(\hat{Y}^{\text{arr}}_{b,h}-\hat{Y}^{\text{nig}}_{b,h})}{S^{\text{arr}}_{\text{base},b,h}}\right)^2.
\end{equation}
The loss penalizes violations during training. After fusion, $\Pi_{\mathcal{C}}$ enforces the lower bound algebraically. Its implementation preserves nonnegative arrivals, so arrivals error should be invariant to projection apart from floating-point precision. This property is checked in the reproduced results.

\subsection{Uncertainty Quantification (Conformal DCQF)}
Split conformal prediction provides finite-sample marginal coverage under exchangeability \cite{angelopoulos2021conformal}. Eurostat residuals can be serially dependent and correlated across overlapping accommodation categories. We therefore measure coverage descriptively. Calibration uses observed paired outcomes from held-out 2023 forecasts, and excludes imputed outcomes from the residual sample.

Conformal calibration is evaluated \textbf{post-projection}: nonconformity scores use feasible predictions $\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_i^{\text{proj}} = \Pi_{\mathcal{C}}(\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_i^{\text{raw}})$:
\begin{equation}
s_i = |y_i - \hat{Y}_i^{\text{proj}}|.
\end{equation}
The conformal quantile $\hat{Q}_{1-\alpha}$ is computed at the finite-sample adjusted level $\lceil (N_{\text{cal}} + 1)(1 - \alpha) \rceil / N_{\text{cal}}$. Calibrated prediction intervals are centered on post-projection point forecasts $\hat{Y}^* = \Pi_{\mathcal{C}}(\hat{Y}_{\text{fused}})$:
\begin{equation}
\hat{\mathcal{I}}_{1-\alpha} = \left[\max\left(0, \hat{Y}^* - \hat{Q}_{1-\alpha}\right), \, \hat{Y}^* + \hat{Q}_{1-\alpha}\right].
\end{equation}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Proposed availability-constrained evaluation protocol, not yet empirically evaluated}
\label{alg:proposed_pit}
\label{alg:pipeline}
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Reference-period splits, forecast origins $o$, and a release-vintage ledger for targets and exogenous streams.
\STATE Build training histories using releases available at each historical issuance time. Fit scalers, fallback statistics, HMM parameters, state ordering, and TRMF on admissible training records.
\FOR{each epoch on $\mathcal{D}_{\text{train}}$}
    \STATE Decompose series into $\mathbf{X}_{\text{trend}}$ and $\mathbf{X}_{\text{seasonal}}$ via kernel $K_{\text{ma}}$.
    \STATE Modulate trend via FiLM($\mathbf{e}_r$); project exogenous streams; compute $\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{\text{temporal}}$.
    \STATE Update parameters via AdamW on coupled loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{MSE}} + \lambda_{\text{cons}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{cons}}$.
\ENDFOR
\STATE Generate validation and calibration predictions as of their origins. Tune $w_{\text{time}}^*$ on validation. Use calibration residuals only after their outcomes have been released.
\FOR{each test forecast origin $o$}
    \STATE Select vintages released by $o$. Construct histories and calendar inputs using $\mathcal{F}_o$. Apply training-fitted preprocessing.
    \STATE Filter HMM signals available by $o$ and propagate beliefs through any unreleased periods and the forecast horizon.
    \STATE Fuse forecasts $\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{\text{fused}}$; apply projection $\hat{\mathbf{Y}}^* = \Pi_{\mathcal{C}}(\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{\text{fused}})$.
\STATE Form conformal intervals using calibration outcomes released by $o$. Log origin, target period, horizon, input vintages, and predictions before scoring outcomes.
\ENDFOR
\RETURN Origin-stamped forecasts and paired Original/PIT scores. This protocol specifies the required re-evaluation.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Temporal Leakage Audit and Proposed Point in Time Evaluation}
\label{sec:availability}

\noindent \textbf{Empirical status.} No paired experiment comparing \emph{Original feature availability} with \emph{Point-in-time constrained availability} has been completed for either dataset. The reported scores use retrospective snapshots indexed by reference period. They do not quantify the effect of publication delays or historical revisions on forecast accuracy. The release-selection rules and Algorithm~\ref{alg:proposed_pit} specify a proposed re-evaluation; they are not an implemented PIT result.

\noindent \textbf{Reference dates and forecast origins.} The feature-store field \texttt{date} is a reference-period label. For example, \texttt{2024-01-01} identifies January 2024 in Eurostat and the year 2024 in an annual panel. The stored schema contains reference dates and values, while historical release timestamps and revision vintages are absent. For the specified point-in-time (PIT) backtest, a forecast block begins at the start of month $t+1$ for Eurostat or year $t+1$ for World Bank, with issuance $o$ set to the final second of the preceding period in UTC. Thus a block beginning January 2024 has origin 31 December 2023 at 23:59:59 UTC. Each scored prediction must retain the entity, origin $o$, target period, and horizon $h$. Predictions from overlapping origins are scored separately by horizon. Any pooled score must declare its aggregation rule.

\noindent \textbf{Release and vintage selection.} For variable $j$, entity $i$, reference period $s$, and vintage $v$, let $r_{i,j,s,v}$ be the timestamp when that version became available through the data source used by the forecasting system. The admissible vintage and value are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
v^*(i,j,s;o)&=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{v:\,r_{i,j,s,v}\le o}r_{i,j,s,v},\\
x_{i,j,s}^{(o)}&=x_{i,j,s,v^*(i,j,s;o)}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:asof_selection}
\end{equation}
If the eligible set is empty, the input is unavailable at $o$. This rule also applies to historical tourism targets used as predictors. The release ledger must record the variable and source identifier, reference period, release timestamp, vintage identifier, and retrieval timestamp. Retrieval time documents provenance. Admissibility depends on the release of the particular vintage. A field named \texttt{lag1} specifies a reference-period lag and still requires this release check.

Eurostat currently describes country-level monthly accommodation releases as generally occurring soon after a 45-day transmission deadline, with EU aggregates usually released about 2.5 months after the reference month \cite{eurostat_tourism_release}. Full HICP breakdowns are generally released around the middle of the following month, while the earlier flash estimate covers selected aggregates \cite{eurostat_hicp_release}. The accommodation-price component must use its own release. These schedules motivate availability checks. Historical backtests require the release applicable to each country, component, and date. For annual GDP, growth, inflation, unemployment, and exchange-rate variables, release timing must likewise be obtained for the specific source series. WDI historical values can change with updates, and archived WDI editions provide a basis for reconstructing available vintages \cite{worldbank_revisions,worldbank_archives}.

\noindent \textbf{Unavailable inputs and preprocessing.} PIT construction starts with raw, timestamped releases before imputation. Forward filling may use the latest value already released for that entity. Initial gaps may use an entity or pooled fallback estimated from admissible training data. A delayed tourism period requires an explicit missing-history or origin-specific nowcast treatment, with its status retained in the forecast log. Only published outcomes serve as training or calibration labels. Backward filling and interpolation with later values are excluded from this protocol. Scalers, clipping thresholds, historical accommodation shares, economic ratios, and TRMF factors must be fitted on the admissible training partition. The available loader computes some historical ratios on the complete panel, which creates an additional source of future information. Unavailable covariates without an auditable release history are omitted, and the resulting feature set is disclosed. Lagged latest-vintage sensitivity runs can be reported when release records are incomplete, with their lag assumptions stated explicitly. Such runs measure delay sensitivity while leaving revision uncertainty unresolved.

\noindent \textbf{Regime information at issuance.} The HMM filtering procedure in Section~\ref{sec:regime} is a requirement of the specified PIT evaluation. Each training example uses a state estimate from the information available at its historical origin. At validation and test origins, the fitted HMM and its normalization are fixed. Newly released measurements update the filtered distribution, and transitions propagate it across unobserved periods. A future COVID restriction, a realized annual stringency average, or the arrival total of a target month enters the regime calculation only after its release. Future deterministic calendars remain admissible. Future observed macroeconomic values require a separate forecast produced using the same $\mathcal{F}_o$ and logged as a forecast input.

\noindent \textbf{Required paired comparison and current status.} The \emph{Original feature availability} setting retains the retrospective feature store, imputation, and regime construction underlying the original benchmark configuration. The \emph{Point-in-time constrained} setting applies the release, vintage, preprocessing, and filtering rules above. Both settings must use the same declared reference-period partitions, forecast origins, horizons, scoring targets, tuning budget, and seed policy. A common evaluation vintage and target-validity mask are fixed for scoring after outcomes are released. Each setting is refitted and tuned on its own admissible training and validation inputs. Calibration residuals become eligible only when their outcomes are released before test issuance. If delayed calibration outcomes are unavailable at the first test origin, the calibration window must end earlier or be accumulated sequentially, with the same declared rule across models.

For each target, horizon, and regime, the paired experiment must report WAPE, MAE, the number of origins and scored outcomes, and differences
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_{\mathrm{WAPE}}&=\mathrm{WAPE}_{\mathrm{PIT}}-\mathrm{WAPE}_{\mathrm{Original}},\\
\Delta_{\mathrm{MAE}}&=\mathrm{MAE}_{\mathrm{PIT}}-\mathrm{MAE}_{\mathrm{Original}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:availability_difference}
\end{equation}
WAPE differences are expressed in percentage points. Relative degradation for metric $M$ is $100(M_{\mathrm{PIT}}/M_{\mathrm{Original}}-1)\%$ when $M_{\mathrm{Original}}>0$. Positive differences indicate higher error under the information restriction. Paired uncertainty estimates must resample the same chronological origin blocks in both settings. All models are scored on common eligible target keys, and exclusions caused by missing release histories are counted separately.

The Eurostat tables in this manuscript report the newly reproduced reference-period experiment. That runner fits HMM parameters and normalization within training and uses forward-filtered origin states, while the original unified pipeline retains the information exposures identified above. This corrected runner is a separate reference-period configuration, not the PIT arm of a matched availability experiment. Differences between the earlier benchmark scores and the reproduced scores also reflect model, preprocessing, and evaluation changes; they cannot be interpreted as leakage-related degradation. The feature store still lacks the release ledger and vintage snapshots required for a paired PIT experiment. PIT WAPE, PIT MAE, and all Original--PIT differences therefore remain unmeasured. A fixed-lag experiment on the latest-vintage snapshot could measure sensitivity to assumed publication delays, but would not resolve historical revisions or substitute for a full PIT comparison. Each reproduced run records its input hash, split boundaries, code hashes, seed, and keyed forecasts, providing a reference for the required release-aware re-evaluation.

\subsection{Length of Stay and Joint Target Errors}
\label{sec:los_analysis}
For matching Eurostat destination, accommodation category, and month, define
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{LOS}_{i,t}=\frac{Y^{\mathrm{nig}}_{i,t}}{Y^{\mathrm{arr}}_{i,t}},
\qquad
\widehat{\operatorname{LOS}}_{i,t}=
\frac{\hat{Y}^{\mathrm{nig}}_{i,t}}{\hat{Y}^{\mathrm{arr}}_{i,t}}.
\label{eq:los_ratio}
\end{equation}
This ratio measures nights per registered accommodation arrival in a destination, category, and month. We score MAE and RMSE in nights per arrival using observed pairs with positive actual arrivals. A zero predicted-arrivals denominator makes forecast LOS undefined. Each paired comparison uses the country-months with defined ratios for both DLinear and the full model. The complete-sample comparison reports each model's coverage separately.

Let $\epsilon_A=\hat{Y}^{\mathrm{arr}}-Y^{\mathrm{arr}}$ and $\epsilon_N=\hat{Y}^{\mathrm{nig}}-Y^{\mathrm{nig}}$. For positive actual and predicted arrivals, the exact identity
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\operatorname{LOS}}-\operatorname{LOS}
=\frac{\epsilon_N-\operatorname{LOS}\,\epsilon_A}{\hat{Y}^{\mathrm{arr}}}
\label{eq:los_error_decomposition}
\end{equation}
shows that proportional count errors can cancel in the ratio, while a small predicted-arrivals denominator can amplify ratio error. Good LOS accuracy therefore does not guarantee accurate visitor or overnight-demand counts, and lower count errors need not improve their ratio.

\input{tables/reviewer_los.tex}

On the 1,122 recent aggregate country-months scored for both models, LOS MAE is 0.155 for RA-HE-DLinear and 0.182 for DLinear. DLinear predicts zero arrivals for seven of the 1,129 eligible cases. On all 1,129 cases, the full model has LOS MAE of 0.172 and RMSE of 0.417. Seasonal naive also covers the full sample and has lower MAE and RMSE of 0.095 and 0.245. On the recovery fold's paired sample, the full model has higher LOS MAE than DLinear, at 0.352 versus 0.222.

The pandemic fold illustrates the sensitivity of LOS RMSE to small denominators. For Iceland in March 2021, DLinear predicts about 50 arrivals and 12,799 nights, giving an absolute ratio error of about 256 nights per arrival. That country-month contributes 98.0\% of DLinear's squared LOS error on the 758 paired cases. In the recent aggregate sample, the mean predicted LOS is 2.601 versus 2.588 observed, a bias of $+0.013$ nights per arrival. Signed pooled biases are $-10.94\%$ for arrivals and $-12.92\%$ for nights. The ratio bias gives each country-month equal weight, while the pooled count biases weight by volume.

The recent raw and projected LOS errors coincide to reported precision on the aggregate category. The maximum ratio change is below $6\times10^{-7}$ nights per arrival. Full-model LOS MAE is 0.130 for high-income and 0.358 for upper-middle-income destinations. Montenegro contributes 44.1\% of the latter group's absolute ratio error. Leaving it out in a sensitivity check reduces that group's MAE to 0.235, while the primary comparison includes all seven destinations. This concentration limits the interpretation of the group difference.

\noindent \textbf{Trend Kernel Ablation on Short Annual Panels:} Lookback $L=4$ with monthly default $K_{\text{ma}}=13$ requires edge endpoint replication. Ablating across $K_{\text{ma}} \in \{13, 3, 1\}$ reveals high parameter stability: overall arrivals WAPE is $30.49\%$ ($K=13$), $30.41\%$ ($K=3$), and $30.41\%$ ($K=1$, un-decomposed Linear LTSF), with test RMSE improving from $12.49\text{M}$ to $12.30\text{M}$. Thus, compact kernels ($K_{\text{ma}}=3$) or pure linear mappings ($K_{\text{ma}}=1$) are methodologically preferable for short annual panels.

\section{Discussion and Tourism Management Implications}
\label{sec:management}

\noindent \textbf{Model choice across tourism contexts.} In the recent Eurostat fold, seasonal naive and the DLinear control have lower count errors than RA-HE-DLinear. The factorial comparisons describe the changes obtained by enabling specific temporal components under the fixed fusion rule. The calendar folds and destination groups show how forecasting errors vary with demand conditions and volume.

\noindent \textbf{Three recovery cases.} We select the destination at the median 2023/2019 arrivals ratio within each recovery tercile. This rule uses pre-test recovery information and produces three cases with different recovery levels. Table~\ref{tab:reviewer_cases} and Figures~\ref{fig:tourism_cases}--\ref{fig:los_cases} show observed demand, DLinear/full forecast errors, and direct LOS errors.

\input{tables/reviewer_cases.tex}
\input{tables/reviewer_case_discussion.tex}

For a destination management organization, an arrivals projection describes accommodation registrations, while a nights projection describes corresponding accommodation-night demand. A projected rise in arrivals combined with a falling LOS implies a different overnight-demand profile from the same arrivals growth with stable LOS. This can inform scenario discussions about seasonal staffing and accommodation demand. Converting these scenarios into room capacity or staffing decisions additionally requires occupancy, room supply, productivity, costs, and service-level data.

\noindent \textbf{Using the income and LOS diagnostics.} Seasonal naive supplies a reference for both income groups. The LOS diagnostic checks how registration and overnight-demand predictions relate at a destination. For Montenegro, the recent full-model mean predicted monthly LOS is 4.068 nights per arrival against 3.009 observed. This discrepancy illustrates why separate arrivals and nights errors, ratio bias, denominator coverage, and local seasonal patterns matter when interpreting a forecast for planning.

Nominal 90\% intervals show the range of historical calibration residuals around each forecast. A broad nights interval can motivate contingency scenarios and flexible resource commitments, while local point-error comparisons determine which forecast supplies those scenarios. Pooled interval width and achieved coverage are reported with the figures. This study measures forecasting and interval behavior. A separate decision experiment would be needed to quantify improvements in capacity utilization or allocation efficiency.

\noindent \textbf{Limitations.} The Eurostat benchmark uses one seed and January origins with a 12-month horizon. The three-seed factorial covers the recent fold, with no HMM state transition across its test origins within each seed. Its dispersion estimates describe seed variability on that fold. The income comparison scores 30 high-income and seven upper-middle-income destinations with observed outcomes in the recent fold. Volume weighting and undefined ratios affect pooled comparisons. The final block includes seven months, and the aggregate accommodation category is used for country diagnostics. A separate release-vintage backtest is required to quantify operational errors under publication delays and revisions. The annual World Bank table reports a separate retrospective reference.

\section{Conclusion}
The rerun Eurostat benchmark evaluates tourism-specific baselines, DLinear controls, and calendar stress folds. On \ReviewerObservedPairs{} recent observed pairs, RA-HE-DLinear records WAPE of \ReviewerArrivalWAPE{}\% for arrivals and \ReviewerNightsWAPE{}\% for nights. Seasonal naive and DLinear have lower WAPE for both targets. With the same validation gate and spatial forecast within each seed, the direct exogenous projections lower fused WAPE for both targets. The HMM effects are smaller, and multiplicative FiLM raises both errors under the current training protocol. Income-stratified results and errors in the nights-to-arrivals ratio describe performance across destinations. Operational evaluation requires the original historical publication and revision vintages.
