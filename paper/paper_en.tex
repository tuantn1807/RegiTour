\title{RA-HE-DLinear: A Regime-Aware Hierarchical-Economic Linear Framework for Multi-Country Tourism Demand Forecasting}

\author{Nguyen-Tuan~Tran,
        Hoai-Bao~Phan}

\IEEEtitleabstractindextext{%
\begin{abstract}
Accurate multi-horizon forecasting of international tourism demand, measured by tourist arrivals ($Y_{\text{arr}}$) and overnight stays ($Y_{\text{nig}}$), provides the quantitative foundation for destination carrying capacity planning, hotel room inventory allocation, and national macroeconomic policy. Recently, linear decomposition architectures (LTSF-Linear, notably DLinear) have demonstrated superior computational efficiency compared to complex deep recurrent and attention networks. However, when deployed on volatile empirical tourism panels, standard DLinear exhibits three core methodological limitations: (1) an endogenous-only formulation lacking mechanisms to ingest deterministic future calendar schedules and historical macroeconomic indicators; (2) static trend projection weights invariant to structural breaks (such as the COVID-19 disruption); and (3) independent univariate modeling that violates domain business consistency between arrivals and overnight stays. This paper proposes \textbf{RA-HE-DLinear} (\textit{Regime-Aware Hierarchical-Economic DLinear}), a unified spatial-temporal linear decomposition framework resolving these limitations. At its core, RA-HE-DLinear incorporates the \textbf{RA-Exo-DLinear} temporal engine, inheriting moving-average decomposition while generalizing under Generalized Additive Models (GAM): it introduces Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM) conditioned on Hidden Markov Model (HMM) macroeconomic regimes on the trend branch and orthogonal dual-stream projections for future calendar schedules and past macro shocks with learnable scaling parameters. RA-HE-DLinear couples this temporal core with low-rank Temporal Regularized Matrix Factorization (TRMF) to capture borderless spatial spillovers across countries, an asymmetric anchor consistency solver ($\text{Nights} \ge \text{Arrivals} \times \text{LOS}_{\min}$), and deep conformal calibration (DCQF) for distribution-free uncertainty intervals. Empirical evaluation on a monthly Eurostat panel of 38 European countries (2010--2026, 4,712 test observations) under strict chronological evaluation protocols demonstrates that RA-HE-DLinear achieves a Weighted Absolute Percentage Error (WAPE) of \textbf{14.59\% $\pm$ 0.46\%} on arrivals and \textbf{25.00\% $\pm$ 0.54\%} on overnight stays. It maintains an optimal historical economic band deviation rate of \textbf{16.02\%} (significantly outperforming LightGBM at 49.19\% and Seasonal Naive at 28.82\%) while eliminating physical lower-bound violations to strictly \textbf{0.00\%} post-projection with a minimal pre-projection intervention rate of only \textbf{0.15\%} (7/4,712 samples). On a global annual panel of 203 economies from the World Bank (2018--2020), RA-HE-DLinear achieves an arrivals WAPE of \textbf{9.45\% $\pm$ 0.38\%} and overnight stays WAPE of \textbf{28.27\% $\pm$ 0.82\%}, confirming robust multi-frequency adaptability.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Background and Motivation}
\IEEEPARstart{I}{n} tourism economics and hospitality operations, multi-horizon forecasting of tourist arrivals ($Y_{\text{arr}}$) and overnight stays ($Y_{\text{nig}}$) provides the empirical basis for airline yield management, hotel inventory planning, and national fiscal policy \cite{song2021tourism}. Accurate forecasts across multi-step planning horizons ($H \in \{3, 6, 12\}$ months) enable Destination Management Organizations (DMOs) and governments to avoid severe supply shortages during peak seasons while mitigating excessive operational overhead during demand contractions.

Over the past four decades, forecasting methodologies have advanced from classical econometrics (SARIMA, Holt-Winters) \cite{athanasopoulos2011tourism}, tabular machine learning (LightGBM, CatBoost) \cite{dorogush2018catboost}, to deep recurrent and attention networks (LSTM, Transformer) \cite{claveria2016tourism, silva2023tourism}. Recently, Zeng et al. (AAAI 2023) \cite{zeng2023dlinear} introduced \textbf{DLinear}, demonstrating that simple moving-average decomposition combined with channel-independent linear mappings can outperform sophisticated Transformer architectures on long-term time series forecasting (LTSF) benchmarks with orders-of-magnitude lower computational overhead.

\subsection{Problem Formulation and Research Challenges}
Despite its merits, empirical deployment of standard DLinear on real-world multi-country tourism panels spanning 2010--2026 exposes three foundational methodological shortcomings:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Exogenous Blindness:} The standard DLinear architecture \cite{zeng2023dlinear} is strictly an autoregressive, endogenous mapping operating solely on historical target lags. In tourism econometrics, travel demand is fundamentally driven by exogenous covariates \cite{peng2014meta}. Specifically, future deterministic calendar variations (religious festivities, school holiday schedules, bridge weekend distributions) are known with certainty across horizon $H$ ($\mathbf{X}_{\text{fut}}$), while past macroeconomic disruptions—such as HICP accommodation inflation and public health restriction stringency indices \cite{hale2021oxford} ($\mathbf{X}_{\text{past}}$)—exert significant elasticity on tourist flows.
    \item \textbf{Static Trend Projections under Structural Breaks:} The stationary linear trend mapping $\mathbf{W}_T \mathbf{X}_{\text{trend}}$ in DLinear \cite{zeng2023dlinear} presumes a time-invariant data generating process. Econometric theory proves that unmodeled structural breaks represent the single primary catalyst for deterministic linear forecast failure \cite{clements1998forecasting}. Under extreme exogenous shocks—such as the COVID-19 pandemic, which precipitated a catastrophic $>70\%$ contraction in international tourist arrivals \cite{gossling2020pandemics}—static projection weights saturate and lag catastrophically due to the absence of regime-switching mechanisms \cite{hamilton1989regime}.
    \item \textbf{Domain Inconsistency across Co-Dependent Targets:} Independent univariate modeling across co-dependent target series breaches structural reconciliation principles \cite{hyndman2011optimal, cortes2024inconsistency}. Standard regressors frequently output contradictory trajectories where projected overnight stays fall below tourist arrivals ($\hat{Y}_{\text{nig}} < \hat{Y}_{\text{arr}}$), implying an average length of stay ($\text{LOS} = \hat{Y}_{\text{nig}}/\hat{Y}_{\text{arr}}$) strictly below $1.0$ nights. Under official international accounting standards set forth by UN Tourism \cite{unwto2010recommendations} and Eurostat Regulation (EU) No 692/2011, every registered arrival at a collective accommodation establishment strictly generates at least one overnight stay ($\text{LOS} \ge 1.0$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Research Objectives and Contributions}
To address these limitations, this study proposes \textbf{RA-HE-DLinear} (\textit{Regime-Aware Hierarchical-Economic DLinear}), a unified spatial-temporal linear decomposition framework. The principal scientific contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Core Temporal-Exogenous Engine (RA-Exo-DLinear):} Formulating RA-Exo-DLinear under a Generalized Additive Model (GAM) formulation. It introduces Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM) conditioned on Hidden Markov Model (HMM) macroeconomic regimes on the trend branch and orthogonal dual-stream projections for future calendar and past macro shocks with learnable scaling parameters.
    \item \textbf{Graph-Free Spatial Factorization (TRMF):} Integrating low-rank Temporal Regularized Matrix Factorization to capture cross-country tourism spillovers (Schengen area mobility) without imposing subjective geographic distance graphs.
    \item \textbf{Asymmetric Anchor Consistency Solver ($\Pi_{\mathcal{C}}$):} Developing an asymmetric projection method anchoring on primary observed border registration counts combined with a coupled regularized training loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{cons}}$). This bounds the physical lower limit ($\text{Nights} \ge \text{Arrivals}$) while restricting raw unprojected physical violations to just 0.15\% (7/4,712 samples) and reducing historical country-specific economic band deviations to 16.02\% (versus 49.19\% for LightGBM).
    \item \textbf{Distribution-Free Uncertainty Quantification (DCQF):} Integrating Deep Conformal Quantile Forecasting, delivering finite-sample prediction intervals $[P_{10}, P_{90}]$ with theoretical coverage guarantees $\ge 90\%$ without distributional assumptions.
    \item \textbf{Comprehensive Multi-Panel Empirical Validation:} Evaluating on two real-world datasets: monthly Eurostat panel (38 countries) and annual World Bank panel (203 economies) under strict chronological protocols, reporting bootstrap Mean $\pm$ Std across 1,000 iterations, ablation breakdowns, and sensitivity curves.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Tourism Demand Forecasting}
Classical econometrics such as SARIMA and Error Correction Models (ECM) dominated tourism literature for decades \cite{athanasopoulos2011tourism, song2021tourism}. In recent years, tabular machine learning (LightGBM, CatBoost) \cite{dorogush2018catboost} and deep sequence models (LSTM) \cite{claveria2016tourism, silva2023tourism} gained popularity due to non-linear flexibility. However, these models either rely on manual lag engineering or suffer from internal state saturation when facing severe macroeconomic shocks.

\subsection{Regime-Aware Time-Series Forecasting}
Concept drift and structural breaks are pervasive challenges in macroeconomic and tourism series \cite{clements1998forecasting}. The Markov-Switching regime model developed by Hamilton \cite{hamilton1989regime} established the standard for identifying latent economic phases. Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM) \cite{perez2018film} provides an efficient mechanism to condition neural layers on external context without adding structural depth. However, existing econometric regime-switching frameworks are rarely integrated directly into modern linear decomposition backbones.

\subsection{Multivariate and Cross-Country Forecasting}
In tightly integrated geographical markets such as the European Schengen area, tourism flows exhibit significant spatial cross-correlations. While Spatio-Temporal Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) require ad-hoc geographical adjacency matrices, Temporal Regularized Matrix Factorization (TRMF \cite{yu2016trmf}) models multi-country dependencies via low-rank latent factor decomposition regularized by autoregressive temporal penalties. Nonetheless, cross-country dependency modeling has historically lacked coordination with multi-target business constraints.

\subsection{Uncertainty Quantification and Explainability}
Point forecasts lack the confidence bounds required for destination risk mitigation. Conformal prediction \cite{vovk2005algorithmic, angelopoulos2021conformal} provides distribution-free finite-sample coverage guarantees. Simultaneously, SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations \cite{lundberg2017shap}) enables attribution analysis to interpret model predictions. However, previous tourism demand studies rarely combine conformal coverage guarantees with business consistency projection and feature interpretability.

\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Problem Formulation and Mathematical Notation}
Consider a tourism panel of $N$ countries observed over time $t = 1, \dots, T$. For each entity $i$ at time $t$, the target vector is $\mathbf{y}_{i, t} = [Y_{i, t}^{\text{arr}}, Y_{i, t}^{\text{nig}}]^T \in \mathbb{R}_+^2$, denoting tourist arrivals and overnight stays. Given a lookback window $L$ and forecast horizon $H$, the objective is to estimate:
\begin{equation}
\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{i, t+1:t+H} = \mathcal{M}\left(\mathbf{Y}_{i, t-L+1:t}, \, \mathbf{X}_{i, t-L+1:t}^{\text{past}}, \, \mathbf{X}_{i, t+1:t+H}^{\text{fut}}, \, \mathbf{s}_t\right)
\end{equation}
subject to the physical lower-bound constraint:
\begin{equation}
\hat{Y}_{i, \tau}^{\text{nig}} \ge \hat{Y}_{i, \tau}^{\text{arr}} \times \text{LOS}_{\min}, \quad \forall \tau \in [t+1, t+H]
\end{equation}
where $\text{LOS}_{\min} = 1.0$ nights.

The pipeline operates across six integrated blocks:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Market Regime Navigator (HMM):} Classifies macroeconomic conditions into three regimes: Normal Growth, Disruption Shock, and Recovery.
    \item \textbf{Series Decomposition:} Decomposes past endogenous series into $\mathbf{X}_{\text{trend}}$ and $\mathbf{X}_{\text{seasonal}}$ via average pooling with kernel $K_{\text{ma}}$.
    \item \textbf{Regime-Aware Temporal Engine (RA-Exo-DLinear):} Applies FiLM modulation to the trend branch and projects dual exogenous streams $(\mathbf{X}_{\text{fut}}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{past}})$.
    \item \textbf{Cross-Country Spatial Engine (TRMF):} Captures cross-country spatial spillovers via low-rank matrix decomposition ($R=4$).
    \item \textbf{Spatio-Temporal Fusion and Consistency Solver ($\Pi_{\mathcal{C}}$):} Fuses temporal and spatial signals, applying projection $\Pi_{\mathcal{C}}$ to ensure $\hat{Y}_{\text{nig}} \ge \hat{Y}_{\text{arr}}$.
    \item \textbf{Uncertainty Quantification (Conformal DCQF):} Calibrates distribution-free prediction intervals $[P_{10}, P_{90}]$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Proposed RA-HE-DLinear Formulation}
\noindent \textbf{Methodological Demarcation:} The core innovation lies in the \textit{unified integration and mathematical formulation} of moving-average decomposition, regime-conditioned modulation, low-rank spatial spillovers, and asymmetric domain projection into a single coherent pipeline. Rather than claiming HMM, TRMF, or Conformal Prediction as standalone inventions, RA-HE-DLinear resolves the operational barriers that previously prevented linear time-series models from maintaining consistency across multi-country macroeconomic shocks.

\subsection{Regime-Aware Temporal Modeling (RA-Exo-DLinear)}
\noindent \textbf{Input:} Endogenous historical lags $\mathbf{X}$, future calendar schedule $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fut}}$, historical macroeconomic covariates $\mathbf{X}_{\text{past}}$, and market regime $s_t$.

\noindent \textbf{Processing:} Following Generalized Additive Models (GAM \cite{hastie1990gam}):
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{X}_{\text{trend}} = \text{AvgPool}(\mathbf{X}, K_{\text{ma}}), \quad \mathbf{X}_{\text{seasonal}} = \mathbf{X} - \mathbf{X}_{\text{trend}}
\end{equation}
Seasonality is mapped via stationary weights $\mathbf{W}_S \mathbf{X}_{\text{seasonal}}$. The trend branch absorbs macroeconomic shifts via FiLM \cite{perez2018film}:
\begin{equation}
f_{\text{trend}}(\mathbf{X}_{\text{trend}}, \mathbf{s}_t) = \boldsymbol{\gamma}(\mathbf{e}_r) \odot (\mathbf{W}_T \mathbf{X}_{\text{trend}}) + \boldsymbol{\beta}(\mathbf{e}_r)
\end{equation}
with bounded scaling parameters:
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\gamma}(\mathbf{e}_r) = 1.0 + \alpha_{\text{trend}} \cdot \tanh(\mathbf{W}_\gamma \mathbf{e}_r + \mathbf{b}_\gamma), \quad \boldsymbol{\beta}(\mathbf{e}_r) = \mathbf{W}_\beta \mathbf{e}_r + \mathbf{b}_\beta
\end{equation}
Future calendar variables and historical shocks are projected orthogonally:
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\text{fut}} = \alpha_{\text{fut}} \cdot \tanh(\operatorname{Linear}(\mathbf{X}_{\text{fut}})), \quad \boldsymbol{\delta}_{\text{past}} = \alpha_{\text{past}} \cdot \tanh(\operatorname{Linear}(\mathbf{X}_{\text{past}}))
\end{equation}
Raw point predictions are computed via first-order Taylor expansion around baseline scale $S_{\text{base}}$:
\begin{equation}
\hat{Y}_{\text{raw}} = \operatorname{ReLU}\left(S_{\text{base}} \cdot \left[1.0 + \delta_{\text{trend}} + \delta_{\text{fut}} + \delta_{\text{past}}\right]\right)
\end{equation}

\noindent \textbf{Contribution:} Overcomes exogenous blindness and enables trend slopes to adapt smoothly across market crises.

\subsection{Cross-Country Dependency Modeling (TRMF)}
\noindent \textbf{Input:} Panel observation matrix $\mathbf{Y} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times T}$ across all countries.

\noindent \textbf{Processing:} Low-rank Temporal Regularized Matrix Factorization \cite{yu2016trmf} decomposes $\mathbf{Y}$ into loading matrix $\mathbf{W} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times R}$ and temporal factors $\mathbf{X}_{\text{sp}} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times R}$:
\begin{equation}
\min_{\mathbf{W}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{sp}}, \boldsymbol{\Theta}} \sum_{(i, t) \in \Omega} (Y_{i, t} - \mathbf{w}_i^T \mathbf{x}_t)^2 + \lambda_w \|\mathbf{W}\|_F^2 + \lambda_x \mathcal{R}_{\text{AR}}(\mathbf{X}_{\text{sp}}, \boldsymbol{\Theta})
\end{equation}
with autoregressive penalty $\mathcal{R}_{\text{AR}}$ across lag set $\mathcal{L} = \{1, 2, 12\}$. The decomposition rank is configured to $R=4$ reflecting the official United Nations UN M49 / Eurostat geographic division of European tourism into four macro-regions (Western, Northern, Southern, and Eastern Europe), and is post-hoc verified via PCA/SVD spectrum analysis where the top-4 principal components capture $88.50\%$ of total empirical variance across the 38 economies.

Spatial signals are convexly combined with the temporal engine:
\begin{equation}
\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{\text{fused}} = w_{\text{time}} \hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{\text{temporal}} + (1 - w_{\text{time}}) \hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{\text{spatial}}
\end{equation}
To prevent data leakage and avoid overfitting to transient post-crisis disruptions, the blending coefficient $w_{\text{time}}$ is estimated via domain-prior constrained convex optimization on the held-out validation set $\mathcal{D}_{\text{val}}$ (year 2023):
\begin{equation}
w_{\text{time}}^* = \arg\min_{w \in [w_{\min}, 1.0]} \mathcal{L}_{\text{val}}\left(\mathbf{Y}_{\text{val}}, \, \Pi_{\mathcal{C}}\left(w \hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{\text{temporal}}^{\text{val}} + (1 - w) \hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{\text{spatial}}^{\text{val}}\right)\right)
\end{equation}
Grounded in tourism econometric principles \cite{song2021tourism, peng2014meta} establishing that domestic calendar schedules and endogenous destination carrying capacity govern $\ge 70\%$ of aggregate variance, the search space enforces $w_{\min} = 0.70$. The optimization converges to $w_{\text{time}}^* = 0.70$ on validation data, which is locked for all inference on the unseen test set $\mathcal{D}_{\text{test}}$ (2024--2026).

\noindent \textbf{Contribution:} Exploits cross-border spillovers across Schengen economies without requiring artificial distance matrices.

\subsection{Economic Constraint Projection ($\Pi_{\mathcal{C}}$)}
\noindent \textbf{Input:} Raw prediction tuple $(\hat{Y}_{\text{arr}}, \hat{Y}_{\text{nig}})$.

\noindent \textbf{Processing:} Defining the feasible economic cone:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{C} = \left\{(y_{\text{arr}}, y_{\text{nig}}) \in \mathbb{R}_+^2 \;\middle|\; y_{\text{nig}} \ge y_{\text{arr}} \times \text{LOS}_{\min} \right\}
\end{equation}
Because border arrivals are physical registration anchors, the asymmetric projector $\Pi_{\mathcal{C}}$ fixes $\hat{Y}_{\text{arr}}$ and adjusts overnight stays:
\begin{equation}
\Pi_{\mathcal{C}}(\hat{Y}_{\text{arr}}, \hat{Y}_{\text{nig}}) = \left(\hat{Y}_{\text{arr}}, \, \max\left(\hat{Y}_{\text{nig}}, \, \hat{Y}_{\text{arr}} \times 1.0\right)\right)
\end{equation}
During neural training, a coupled regularization loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{MSE}} + \lambda_{\text{cons}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{cons}}$ penalizes boundary violations:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{cons}} = \frac{1}{B} \sum_{b=1}^B \operatorname{ReLU}\left(\hat{Y}_{b}^{\text{arr}} \times 1.0 - \hat{Y}_{b}^{\text{nig}}\right)^2
\end{equation}

\noindent \textbf{Contribution:} Enforces physical lower bounds without degrading accuracy while controlling raw pre-projection violations to 0.15\%.

\subsection{Uncertainty Quantification (Conformal DCQF)}
Deep Conformal Quantile Forecasting (DCQF) \cite{angelopoulos2021conformal} constructs finite-sample intervals. On validation set $\mathcal{D}_{\text{cal}} = \{(\mathbf{x}_i, y_i)\}_{i=1}^{n_{\text{cal}}}$, preliminary quantiles $\hat{q}_{\alpha/2}(\mathbf{x}_i)$ and $\hat{q}_{1-\alpha/2}(\mathbf{x}_i)$ are fit using pinball loss. The non-conformity score is:
\begin{equation}
s_i = \max\left(\hat{q}_{\alpha/2}(\mathbf{x}_i) - y_i, \, y_i - \hat{q}_{1-\alpha/2}(\mathbf{x}_i)\right)
\end{equation}
The conformal calibration quantile $\hat{Q}_{1-\alpha}$ defines the calibrated prediction interval:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{I}_{1-\alpha}(\mathbf{x}_{t+h}) = \left[\hat{q}_{\alpha/2}(\mathbf{x}_{t+h}) - \hat{Q}_{1-\alpha}, \, \hat{q}_{1-\alpha/2}(\mathbf{x}_{t+h}) + \hat{Q}_{1-\alpha}\right]
\end{equation}

\subsection{Training and Prediction Procedure}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:training_inference_en} details the end-to-end training and inference execution.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{RA-HE-DLinear Training and Prediction Procedure}
\label{alg:training_inference_en}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Panel series $\mathbf{Y}$, exogenous streams $(\mathbf{X}_{\text{fut}}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{past}})$, significance level $\alpha$.
\STATE Fit Gaussian HMM for transition matrix $\mathbf{A}$ and regime $\mathbf{s}_t$; fit TRMF factor matrices $(\mathbf{W}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{sp}})$.
\FOR{each training epoch}
    \STATE Decompose $\mathbf{X} \to (\mathbf{X}_{\text{trend}}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{seasonal}})$ via kernel $K_{\text{ma}}$.
    \STATE Modulate trend via FiLM on $\mathbf{e}_r$; project exogenous streams $(\mathbf{X}_{\text{fut}}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{past}})$.
    \STATE Fuse predictions into $\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{\text{fused}}$; update weights via AdamW on loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}}$.
\ENDFOR
\STATE Apply economic anchor projection: $\hat{\mathbf{Y}}^* = \Pi_{\mathcal{C}}(\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{\text{fused}})$.
\STATE Calibrate conformal prediction intervals $[\mathbf{P}_{10}, \mathbf{P}_{90}]$.
\RETURN Point forecasts $\hat{\mathbf{Y}}^*$ and intervals $[\mathbf{P}_{10}, \mathbf{P}_{90}]$.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

RA-HE-DLinear is benchmarked against representative baselines across methodological paradigms:

    \item \textit{Gradient Boosted Decision Trees:} Standard LightGBM \cite{ke2017lightgbm}.

    \item \textit{Deep Recurrent Models:} Classical LSTM (Hochreiter \& Schmidhuber, 1997) \cite{hochreiter1997lstm}.
    \item \textit{Proposed Core and Integrated Framework:} CatBoost-Regime, RA-Exo-DLinear, and RA-HE-DLinear.

\textit{Note on Classical LSTM Performance:} In the benchmark table, Classical LSTM exhibits a high error rate (108.53\% WAPE on arrivals, 113.16\% on overnight stays). Following the profound $>70\%$ COVID-19 contraction, the recurrent hidden states saturated in the absence of explicit frequency decomposition and market regime conditioning, degenerating into near-global-mean constant projections during the post-pandemic recovery phase.

\subsubsection{Evaluation Metrics}
Forecasting accuracy is measured via WAPE (\%), MAE, and RMSE. Domain consistency is evaluated via Physical Violation Rate ($<1.0$), Projection Intervention Rate ($\eta_{\text{proj}}$), and Historical Economic Band Deviation ($[r_{\min}, r_{\max}]$). Uncertainty is evaluated via Empirical Coverage (\%) and Normalized Prediction Interval Width (NPIW). Metrics report Mean $\pm$ Std across 1,000 bootstrap iterations.

\subsubsection{Evaluation Protocols and Leakage Prevention}
To prevent data leakage:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Strict chronological splitting: Train (2010--2021), Calibration/Validation (2022--2023), Out-of-sample Test (2024--2026).
    \item MinMax scalers, HMM transitions, and TRMF spatial factors are estimated strictly on training observations.
    \item Conformal calibration quantiles $\hat{Q}_{1-\alpha}$ and historical country bounds $[r_{\min}, r_{\max}]$ are computed on the validation partition, entirely isolated from test samples.
\end{itemize}

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Main Findings}
The empirical results reveal three core findings:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textit{Efficacy of Extended Linear LTSF:} Simple linear decomposition models, when equipped with exogenous calendar projections and regime modulation, outperform heavy recurrent architectures on macroeconomic time series.
    \item \textit{Two-Tier Nature of Business Consistency:} Bounding physical violations ($\text{LOS} \ge 1.0$) is achievable below 1\% across most baselines; the authentic scientific differentiator lies in maintaining country-specific economic corridors ($[r_{\min}, r_{\max}]$), where RA-HE-DLinear excels.
    \item \textit{Reliable Distribution-Free Uncertainty:} Conformal prediction guarantees valid coverage without relying on rigid Gaussian assumptions.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Mechanistic Contribution of Model Components}
Why does RA-HE-DLinear succeed?
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RA-Exo-DLinear:} Resolves endogenous blindness by ingesting known future calendar features, aligning forecasts with summer demand peaks.
    \item \textbf{HMM + FiLM:} Mitigates structural break failure by scaling trend slopes across market phases, preventing blind extrapolation during crises.
    \item \textbf{TRMF:} Leverages cross-country spatial covariance, transferring recovery signals across adjacent destinations.
    \item \textbf{Projector $\Pi_{\mathcal{C}}$:} Guarantees domain logic without distorting raw probability mass.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Practical Implications}
For destination managers and hospitality operators, RA-HE-DLinear offers an interpretable, lightweight forecasting tool (training under 40 seconds on standard CPU) that guarantees operational feasibility (no contradictory stays-per-arrival projections), establishing a credible foundation for dynamic pricing and inventory budgeting.

\subsection{Study Limitations}
We note three limitations:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textit{Annual Data Sparsity:} The World Bank panel has limited temporal depth per country; conclusions should be generalized carefully to other annual panels lacking quarterly indicators.
    \item \textit{Exchangeability under Extreme Black-Swan Shocks:} Split conformal assumes exchangeability, which may be locally challenged during unprecedented structural disruptions; future work will explore Adaptive Conformal Inference (ACI).
    \item \textit{Absence of Real-Time High-Frequency Signals:} Incorporating high-frequency flight booking data (IATA) or Google Trends search queries could enhance early turning-point detection.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
This study proposed \textbf{RA-HE-DLinear}, a spatial-temporal linear decomposition framework designed to address three core limitations of LTSF-Linear architectures in multi-country tourism demand forecasting. The framework integrates the regime-conditioned RA-Exo-DLinear temporal engine, low-rank TRMF cross-country spillover modeling, an asymmetric anchor consistency solver, and deep conformal uncertainty calibration.

Empirical evaluation on the Eurostat panel (38 countries) and the World Bank panel (203 economies) demonstrates that RA-HE-DLinear substantially improves accuracy (achieving 14.59\% $\pm$ 0.46\% Arrivals WAPE and 25.00\% $\pm$ 0.54\% Nights WAPE), maintains an optimal economic band deviation of 16.02\%, and eliminates physical lower-bound violations with an intervention rate of only 0.15\%.

These findings provide a balanced paradigm reconciling accuracy, computational efficiency, and business consistency. Future investigations will extend the framework toward online adaptive conformal inference and real-time mobility streams.
