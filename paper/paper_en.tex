\title{RA-HE-DLinear: A Regime-Aware Hierarchical-Economic Linear Framework for Multi-Country Tourism Demand Forecasting}

\author{Nguyen-Tuan~Tran,
        Hoai-Bao~Phan}

\IEEEtitleabstractindextext{%
\begin{abstract}
Forecasting international tourism demand across multiple destinations involves substantial challenges driven by strong seasonality, structural market transitions, and cross-country heterogeneity. While linear time-series decomposition models provide significant computational efficiency, they typically lack mechanisms to ingest exogenous covariates, identify macroeconomic market regimes, and model spatial dependencies between destinations. This study proposes \textbf{RA-HE-DLinear} (\textit{Regime-Aware Hierarchical-Economic DLinear}), a unified spatial-temporal forecasting framework integrating market regime identification, exogenous calendar and price streams, cross-country dependency modeling, and domain economic constraints into a single coherent pipeline. Deep Conformal Quantile Forecasting (DCQF) is incorporated for distribution-free uncertainty quantification, while SHAP is employed to analyze feature attributions.
The framework is evaluated on the Eurostat monthly panel and the World Bank annual panel under strict chronological, leakage-free evaluation protocols. On the Eurostat test set, RA-HE-DLinear achieves a Weighted Absolute Percentage Error (WAPE) of \textbf{14.59\% $\pm$ 0.46\%} on tourist arrivals and \textbf{25.00\% $\pm$ 0.54\%} on overnight stays across 1,000 bootstrap iterations, yielding relative error reductions of 46.6\% and 23.6\% over the Seasonal Naive baseline. On the World Bank test panel, the model achieves an arrivals WAPE of \textbf{9.45\% $\pm$ 0.38\%} and an overnight stays WAPE of \textbf{28.27\% $\pm$ 0.82\%}. The domain consistency projection operator resolves all 7 observed physical lower-bound violations across 4,712 test samples, corresponding to a minimal intervention rate of 0.15\%. Component ablation and regime-stratified evaluations indicate that market regime conditioning, exogenous covariates, and cross-country dependencies each contribute meaningfully to predictive accuracy.
These findings demonstrate that jointly modeling market regimes, cross-country dependencies, and domain constraints can simultaneously enhance both accuracy and logical consistency in multi-country tourism demand forecasting.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Background and Motivation}
\IEEEPARstart{I}{n} tourism economics and hospitality operations, forecasting international tourist arrivals ($Y_{\text{arr}}$) and overnight stays ($Y_{\text{nig}}$) provides essential empirical evidence for destination carrying capacity planning, resource allocation, and tourism governance policies \cite{song2021tourism}. Multi-horizon projections across operational horizons such as $H \in \{3, 6, 12\}$ months assist tourism organizations and hospitality providers in preparing for demand fluctuations between peak and off-peak periods, while proactively mitigating infrastructure strain during peak demand seasons.

Over the past four decades, tourism forecasting methodologies have progressed from classical econometrics (SARIMA, Holt-Winters) \cite{athanasopoulos2011tourism}, tabular machine learning (LightGBM, CatBoost) \cite{dorogush2018catboost}, to deep recurrent neural networks and attention-based sequence models (LSTM, Transformer) \cite{claveria2016tourism, silva2023tourism}. Recently, Zeng et al. (AAAI 2023) \cite{zeng2023dlinear} introduced DLinear, a time-series forecasting architecture utilizing trend-seasonal decomposition paired with linear mappings. Their empirical findings showed competitive performance on several long-term time series forecasting (LTSF) benchmarks. By decomposing raw input series into trend-cyclical ($\mathbf{X}_{\text{trend}}$) and seasonal ($\mathbf{X}_{\text{seasonal}}$) components via moving-average pooling, DLinear outperformed complex Transformer architectures on multiple LTSF datasets with minimal computational overhead.

\subsection{Problem Formulation and Research Challenges}
DLinear decomposes input series into trend and seasonal components using a moving-average filter and projects them via linear layers for multi-step forecasting \cite{zeng2023dlinear}. However, it was not originally formulated to ingest latent market regimes, exogenous covariates, and multi-country spatial dependencies within a unified framework. In multi-country tourism demand forecasting, this study focuses on three primary challenges:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Market Regime Transitions:} The relationship between historical series and future demand can shift fundamentally across normal growth, crisis shocks, and recovery phases.
    \item \textbf{Exogenous and Cross-Country Information:} Calendar schedules, macroeconomic indicators, and shared co-movements across interconnected destinations need to be modeled jointly with the target series.
    \item \textbf{Forecast Domain Consistency:} Projections of tourist arrivals and overnight stays must adhere to established domain business accounting principles, alongside providing calibrated measures of predictive uncertainty.
\end{enumerate}

These challenges motivate the development and evaluation of the RA-HE-DLinear framework. The operational scope and potential limitations of DLinear in this setting are examined empirically through comparative experiments on Eurostat and World Bank panels, rather than assumed universally across all time-series domains.

\subsection{Research Objectives and Contributions}
To address these challenges, this study proposes \textbf{RA-HE-DLinear} (\textit{Regime-Aware Hierarchical-Economic DLinear}). The principal contributions of this work are summarized as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Core Temporal-Exogenous Engine (RA-Exo-DLinear):} Formulating RA-Exo-DLinear under a Generalized Additive Model (GAM) formulation. It introduces Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM) conditioned on Hidden Markov Model (HMM) macroeconomic regimes on the trend branch and orthogonal dual-stream projections for future calendar and past macro shocks with learnable scaling parameters.
    \item \textbf{Graph-Free Spatial Factorization (TRMF):} Integrating low-rank Temporal Regularized Matrix Factorization to capture cross-country tourism spillovers (Schengen area mobility) without imposing subjective geographic distance graphs.
    \item \textbf{Asymmetric Anchor Consistency Solver ($\Pi_{\mathcal{C}}$):} Developing an asymmetric projection method anchoring on primary observed border registration counts combined with a coupled regularized training loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{cons}}$). This bounds the physical lower limit ($\text{Nights} \ge \text{Arrivals}$) while restricting raw unprojected physical violations to just 0.15\% (7/4,712 samples) and reducing historical country-specific economic band deviations to 16.02\% (versus 49.19\% for LightGBM).
    \item \textbf{Distribution-Free Uncertainty Quantification (DCQF):} Integrating Deep Conformal Quantile Forecasting, delivering finite-sample prediction intervals $[P_{10}, P_{90}]$ with theoretical coverage guarantees $\ge 90\%$ without distributional assumptions.
    \item \textbf{Comprehensive Multi-Panel Empirical Validation:} Evaluating on two real-world datasets: monthly Eurostat panel (38 countries) and annual World Bank panel (203 economies) under strict chronological protocols, reporting bootstrap Mean $\pm$ Std across 1,000 iterations, ablation breakdowns, and sensitivity curves.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Tourism Demand Forecasting}
Classical econometric techniques such as SARIMA and Error Correction Models (ECM) have served as foundation stones in tourism demand forecasting for decades \cite{athanasopoulos2011tourism, song2021tourism}. Over the past decade, tabular gradient boosting (LightGBM, CatBoost) \cite{dorogush2018catboost} and recurrent neural networks (LSTM) \cite{claveria2016tourism, silva2023tourism} have been widely adopted due to their capacity to capture non-linear lag relationships. These models effectively leverage non-linear interactions and lag features; however, forecasting performance depends heavily on feature engineering, model tuning, and dataset characteristics. When time series undergo structural shifts, the ability of each method to maintain accuracy requires empirical re-evaluation.

\subsection{Regime-Aware Forecasting}
Concept drift and structural breaks present pervasive challenges in economic and tourism time series \cite{clements1998forecasting}. The Markov-Switching regime model introduced by Hamilton \cite{hamilton1989regime} established a robust theoretical framework for characterizing unobserved business cycle phases. More recently, Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM) \cite{perez2018film} was proposed to condition neural layers on external context without increasing model depth. Integrating regime-switching models with linear decomposition architectures to dynamically adapt trend projections represents the methodology examined in this paper.

\subsection{Multivariate and Cross-Country Forecasting}
In tightly integrated tourism markets such as the European Schengen area, visitor flows between countries exhibit notable spatial co-movements. Spatio-Temporal Graph Neural Networks (ST-GNNs) typically require static adjacency matrices based on physical distances, which struggle to reflect low-cost flight mobility patterns. Temporal Regularized Matrix Factorization (TRMF \cite{yu2016trmf}) addresses this limitation by decomposing panels into low-rank latent factors regularized by autoregressive temporal penalties. Nonetheless, cross-country dependency modeling has historically lacked coordination with internal domain consistency constraints among co-dependent tourism indicators.

\subsection{Uncertainty Quantification and Explainability}
Point forecasts lack the confidence bounds required for destination risk mitigation. Conformal prediction \cite{vovk2005algorithmic, angelopoulos2021conformal} provides distribution-free finite-sample coverage guarantees. Simultaneously, SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations \cite{lundberg2017shap}) enables attribution analysis to interpret model predictions. However, previous tourism demand studies rarely combine conformal coverage guarantees with business consistency projection and feature interpretability.

\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Problem Formulation and Mathematical Notation}
Consider a tourism panel of $N$ destinations observed across time $t = 1, \dots, T$. For each destination $i$ at time $t$, the target vector is $\mathbf{y}_{i, t} = [Y_{i, t}^{\text{arr}}, Y_{i, t}^{\text{nig}}]^T \in \mathbb{R}_+^2$, denoting tourist arrivals and overnight stays. Given a lookback window of length $L$ and a forecast horizon $H$, the objective is to estimate:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{i, t+1:t+H} = \mathcal{M}\big( & \mathbf{Y}_{i, t-L+1:t}, \, \mathbf{X}_{i, t-L+1:t}^{\text{past}}, \\
& \mathbf{X}_{i, t+1:t+H}^{\text{fut}}, \, \mathbf{s}_t\big)
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
satisfying the dual objectives of minimizing forecast error and adhering to the domain physical lower-bound constraint:
\begin{equation}
\hat{Y}_{i, \tau}^{\text{nig}} \ge \hat{Y}_{i, \tau}^{\text{arr}} \times \text{LOS}_{\min}, \quad \forall \tau \in [t+1, t+H]
\end{equation}
where $\text{LOS}_{\min} = 1.0$ nights.

\subsection{Proposed RA-HE-DLinear Framework}
Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture} illustrates the end-to-end architecture of the proposed RA-HE-DLinear framework.

\begin{figure*}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth]{figures/ra_he_dlinear_architecture_en.png}
\caption{Overall architecture of the proposed RA-HE-DLinear framework.}
\label{fig:architecture}
\end{figure*}

The pipeline operates across six integrated blocks:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Market Regime Navigator (HMM):} Determines market state probabilities $s_t \in \{\text{Normal}, \text{Disruption}, \text{Recovery}\}$.
    \item \textbf{Series Decomposition:} Decomposes the endogenous series into trend $\mathbf{X}_{\text{trend}}$ and seasonal $\mathbf{X}_{\text{seasonal}}$ components via moving average filtering with kernel $K_{\text{ma}}$.
    \item \textbf{Regime-Aware Temporal Engine (RA-Exo-DLinear):} Applies FiLM modulation to the trend branch and projects dual exogenous streams $(\mathbf{X}_{\text{fut}}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{past}})$.
    \item \textbf{Cross-Country Spatial Engine (TRMF):} Captures cross-country spatial spillovers via low-rank matrix decomposition ($R=4$).
    \item \textbf{Spatio-Temporal Fusion and Consistency Solver ($\Pi_{\mathcal{C}}$):} Fuses temporal and spatial signals, applying projection $\Pi_{\mathcal{C}}$ to ensure $\hat{Y}_{\text{nig}} \ge \hat{Y}_{\text{arr}}$.
    \item \textbf{Uncertainty Quantification (Conformal DCQF):} Calibrates distribution-free prediction intervals $[P_{10}, P_{90}]$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Regime-Aware Temporal Modeling (RA-Exo-DLinear)}
\noindent \textbf{Input:} Endogenous historical series $\mathbf{X}$, future calendar schedule $\mathbf{X}_{\text{fut}}$, historical macroeconomic covariates $\mathbf{X}_{\text{past}}$, and market regime state $s_t$.

\noindent \textbf{Processing:} Inheriting DLinear's moving-average decomposition \cite{zeng2023dlinear}, the input series is partitioned into two components:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{X}_{\text{trend}} = \text{AvgPool}(\mathbf{X}, K_{\text{ma}}), \quad \mathbf{X}_{\text{seasonal}} = \mathbf{X} - \mathbf{X}_{\text{trend}}
\end{equation}
For monthly data, the residual branch can represent recurring within-year patterns. For annual data, $\mathbf{X}_{\text{seasonal}}$ denotes deviations from the smoothed interannual trend; it does not recover monthly or quarterly seasonality that is absent from the observations. The smoothing scale must therefore be interpreted in units of the input frequency.
The seasonal branch is projected via stationary weights $\mathbf{W}_S \mathbf{X}_{\text{seasonal}}$. The trend branch is modulated via FiLM \cite{perez2018film} conditioned on regime embedding $\mathbf{e}_r$:
\begin{equation}
f_{\text{trend}}(\mathbf{X}_{\text{trend}}, \mathbf{s}_t) = \boldsymbol{\gamma}(\mathbf{e}_r) \odot (\mathbf{W}_T \mathbf{X}_{\text{trend}}) + \boldsymbol{\beta}(\mathbf{e}_r)
\end{equation}
with bounded scaling parameters:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\boldsymbol{\gamma}(\mathbf{e}_r) &= 1.0 + \alpha_{\text{trend}} \cdot \tanh(\mathbf{W}_\gamma \mathbf{e}_r + \mathbf{b}_\gamma), \\
\boldsymbol{\beta}(\mathbf{e}_r) &= \mathbf{W}_\beta \mathbf{e}_r + \mathbf{b}_\beta
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Dual exogenous streams $(\mathbf{X}_{\text{fut}}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{past}})$ are projected independently:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\text{fut}} &= \alpha_{\text{fut}} \cdot \tanh(\operatorname{Linear}(\mathbf{X}_{\text{fut}})), \\
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\text{past}} &= \alpha_{\text{past}} \cdot \tanh(\operatorname{Linear}(\mathbf{X}_{\text{past}}))
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Define the temporal forecast before exogenous adjustments as
\begin{equation}
B = f_{\text{trend}}(\mathbf{X}_{\text{trend}},s_t) + \mathbf{W}_S\mathbf{X}_{\text{seasonal}}.
\end{equation}
The linear projections above include their trainable biases. The empirical scale $S_{\text{base}}$ is the training-set median for each destination, accommodation type, and calendar month for monthly data, or the training-set entity median for annual data. It is floored at one to keep normalization defined when the empirical median is zero. The relative temporal deviation is
\begin{equation}
\delta_{\text{trend}} = \frac{B-S_{\text{base}}}{S_{\text{base}}}.
\end{equation}
It includes both DLinear branches and FiLM modulation; it is distinct from the FiLM gain $\boldsymbol{\gamma}-1$. Raw point predictions then have the equivalent forms
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\hat{Y}_{\text{raw}} &= \operatorname{ReLU}\left(B+S_{\text{base}}(\delta_{\text{fut}}+\delta_{\text{past}})\right) \\
&= \operatorname{ReLU}\left(S_{\text{base}}[1+\delta_{\text{trend}}+\delta_{\text{fut}}+\delta_{\text{past}}]\right).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:temporal_forecast}
\end{equation}
The implementation evaluates the additive form directly. Disabling FiLM and both exogenous streams therefore recovers nonnegative DLinear exactly. No separate empirical-baseline ensemble or event-year adjustment is applied after this temporal forecast; projection is performed after spatial fusion.

\subsection{Cross-Country Dependency Modeling (TRMF)}
\noindent \textbf{Input:} Panel observation matrix $\mathbf{Y} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times T}$ across all $N$ countries.

\noindent \textbf{Processing:} Dual TRMF \cite{yu2016trmf} decomposes the panel observation matrices independently for both tourist arrivals $\mathbf{Y}_{\text{arr}} \approx \mathbf{W}_{\text{arr}} \mathbf{X}_{\text{arr}}^T$ (modeling visitor volume spillovers) and overnight stays $\mathbf{Y}_{\text{nig}} \approx \mathbf{W}_{\text{nig}} \mathbf{X}_{\text{nig}}^T$ (learning destination Length of Stay duration patterns across clusters without assuming a linear scalar ratio):
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\min_{\mathbf{W}_k, \mathbf{X}_k, \boldsymbol{\Theta}_k} \sum_{(i, t) \in \Omega} (Y_{k, i, t} - \mathbf{w}_{k, i}^T \mathbf{x}_{k, t})^2 &+ \lambda_w \|\mathbf{W}_k\|_F^2 \\
&+ \lambda_x \mathcal{R}_{\text{AR}}(\mathbf{X}_k, \boldsymbol{\Theta}_k)
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
for $k \in \{\text{arr}, \text{nig}\}$, with autoregressive regularization $\mathcal{R}_{\text{AR}}$ across lag set $\mathcal{L} = \{1, 2, 12\}$. PCA/SVD spectrum analysis indicates that the top-4 principal components account for 88.50\% of total variance across the 38 destinations, supporting the choice of rank $R=4$.

Spatial forecasts are blended convexly with temporal predictions:
\begin{equation}
\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{\text{fused}} = w_{\text{time}} \hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{\text{temporal}} + (1 - w_{\text{time}}) \hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{\text{spatial}}
\end{equation}
To avoid data leakage and prevent overfitting to transient post-crisis noise, blending weight $w_{\text{time}}$ is estimated by constrained optimization on the 2022 blend-tuning subset of the validation partition $\mathcal{D}_{\text{val}}$; the subsequent 2023 subset is reserved for conformal calibration:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
w_{\text{time}}^* = \arg\min_{w \in [0,1]} \mathcal{L}_{\text{val}}\Big( & \mathbf{Y}_{\text{val}}, \\
& \Pi_{\mathcal{C}}\big(w \hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{\text{temporal}}^{\text{val}} + (1 - w) \hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{\text{spatial}}^{\text{val}}\big)\Big)
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The blending weight $w_{\text{time}}$ is optimized over the full convex interval $[0,1]$, comparing both endpoints with the interior solution. In the Eurostat run using the stated 2010--2021 training and 2022--2023 validation periods, the selected value is $w_{\text{time}}^*=0.8937$ (89.37\% temporal and 10.63\% spatial), with a validation objective of 28.06\% (the equally weighted arrivals and overnight-stays WAPE). No lower-bound prior is imposed on the temporal contribution.

\subsection{Economic Constraint Projection ($\Pi_{\mathcal{C}}$)}
\noindent \textbf{Input:} Raw prediction tuple $(\hat{Y}_{\text{arr}}, \hat{Y}_{\text{nig}})$.

\noindent \textbf{Processing:} Define the feasible economic cone:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{C} = \left\{(y_{\text{arr}}, y_{\text{nig}}) \in \mathbb{R}_+^2 \;\middle|\; y_{\text{nig}} \ge y_{\text{arr}} \times \text{LOS}_{\min} \right\}
\end{equation}
where $\text{LOS}_{\min} = 1.0$ nights. In this adjustment, predicted tourist arrivals serve as the invariant anchor while overnight stays are projected onto the feasible domain:
\begin{equation}
\Pi_{\mathcal{C}}(\hat{Y}_{\text{arr}}, \hat{Y}_{\text{nig}}) = \left(\hat{Y}_{\text{arr}}, \, \max\left(\hat{Y}_{\text{nig}}, \, \hat{Y}_{\text{arr}} \times 1.0\right)\right)
\end{equation}
During neural training, the loss is evaluated on the nonnegative raw outputs before any cross-target projection. The coupled objective $\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{MSE}} + \lambda_{\text{cons}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{cons}}$ uses baseline-normalized prediction errors:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{MSE}} = \frac{1}{N_B H}\sum_{b,h}\sum_{k\in\{\text{arr},\text{nig}\}}\left(\frac{\hat{Y}^{k}_{b,h}-Y^{k}_{b,h}}{S^{k}_{\text{base},b,h}}\right)^2,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{cons}} = \frac{1}{N_B H}\sum_{b,h}\left(\frac{\operatorname{ReLU}(\hat{Y}^{\text{arr}}_{b,h}-\hat{Y}^{\text{nig}}_{b,h})}{S^{\text{arr}}_{\text{base},b,h}}\right)^2,
\end{equation}
where $N_B$ is the batch size and $H$ is the forecast horizon. The raw outputs are retained for consistency evaluation; hard clipping is excluded from this training path so that violations can contribute gradients through $\mathcal{L}_{\text{cons}}$.

\noindent \textbf{Empirical Observations:} On the reported test set, 7 out of 4,712 raw forecast samples violate the physical constraint, corresponding to an intervention rate of 0.15\%. Following projection $\Pi_{\mathcal{C}}$, zero samples violate the lower bound. Post-projection WAPE and MAE exhibit slight improvements in the reported benchmark. This reflects the empirical effect on the evaluated dataset and should not be generalized as guaranteeing accuracy improvements in every setting.

\subsection{Uncertainty Quantification (Conformal DCQF)}
Deep Conformal Quantile Forecasting (DCQF) \cite{angelopoulos2021conformal} constructs finite-sample intervals. On validation set $\mathcal{D}_{\text{cal}} = \{(\mathbf{x}_i, y_i)\}_{i=1}^{n_{\text{cal}}}$, preliminary quantiles $\hat{q}_{\alpha/2}(\mathbf{x}_i)$ and $\hat{q}_{1-\alpha/2}(\mathbf{x}_i)$ are fit using pinball loss. The non-conformity score is:
\begin{equation}
s_i = |r_i| = |y_i - \hat{y}_i|.
\end{equation}
The conformal calibration quantile $\hat{Q}_{1-\alpha}$ defines the calibrated prediction interval:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{I}_{1-\alpha}(\mathbf{x}_{t+h}) = \left[\hat{q}_{\alpha/2}(\mathbf{x}_{t+h}) - \hat{Q}_{1-\alpha}, \, \hat{q}_{1-\alpha/2}(\mathbf{x}_{t+h}) + \hat{Q}_{1-\alpha}\right]
\end{equation}
These endpoints are conformal bounds, not $P_{10}$ and $P_{90}$ quantiles. Finite-sample coverage requires exchangeability; temporal distribution shifts must be assessed empirically on the test period.

\subsection{Overall Training and Inference Procedure}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:training_inference} summarizes the end-to-end execution workflow of the RA-HE-DLinear framework.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{RA-HE-DLinear Training and Inference Procedure}
\label{alg:training_inference}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Panel series $\mathbf{Y}$, exogenous streams $(\mathbf{X}_{\text{fut}}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{past}})$, significance level $\alpha$.
\STATE Fit Gaussian HMM to estimate transition matrix $\mathbf{A}$ and regime $\mathbf{s}_t$; fit TRMF matrices $(\mathbf{W}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{sp}})$.
\FOR{each training epoch}
    \STATE Decompose $\mathbf{X} \to (\mathbf{X}_{\text{trend}}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{seasonal}})$ via kernel $K_{\text{ma}}$.
    \STATE Modulate trend via FiLM on $\mathbf{e}_r$; project exogenous streams $(\mathbf{X}_{\text{fut}}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{past}})$.
    \STATE Blend predictions into $\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{\text{fused}}$; update weights via AdamW on loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}}$.
\ENDFOR
\STATE Apply anchor consistency projection: $\hat{\mathbf{Y}}^* = \Pi_{\mathcal{C}}(\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{\text{fused}})$.
\STATE Calibrate conformal intervals $[\mathbf{P}_{10}, \mathbf{P}_{90}]$ via DCQF.
\RETURN Point forecasts $\hat{\mathbf{Y}}^*$ and prediction intervals $[\mathbf{P}_{10}, \mathbf{P}_{90}]$.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsubsection{Evaluation Metrics}
Forecasting accuracy is quantified using WAPE (\%), MAE, and RMSE for each target. Consistency and uncertainty are evaluated via Physical Violation Rate ($<1.0$), Projection Intervention Rate ($\eta_{\text{proj}}$), Historical Economic Band Deviation ($[r_{\min}, r_{\max}]$), Empirical Coverage (\%), and Normalized Prediction Interval Width (NPIW). For models with bootstrap estimates, results are reported as Mean $\pm$ Std across 1,000 resamples of the test partition. The latest Seasonal Naive and Transformer results are single-run point estimates and do not include bootstrap uncertainty.

\subsubsection{Evaluation Protocol and Leakage Prevention}
To ensure objective evaluation and prevent data leakage:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Strict chronological splitting: Train (2010--2021), Calibration/Validation (2022--2023), Out-of-sample Test (2024--2026).
    \item MinMax scalers, HMM transitions, and TRMF spatial factors are estimated strictly on training observations.
    \item Conformal calibration quantiles $\hat{Q}_{1-\alpha}$ and historical country bounds $[r_{\min}, r_{\max}]$ are computed on the validation partition, entirely isolated from test samples.
\end{itemize}

\noindent \textbf{Interpreting the cross-panel difference:} The results do not indicate that annual data are uniformly harder to forecast. Annual aggregation suppresses within-year seasonal fluctuations and can stabilize arrival totals; at the same time, it removes month-level information about holiday timing and seasonal turning points. Although the World Bank model uses a four-year lookback, this provides only four annual observations per input window, compared with 24 monthly observations for Eurostat, and the World Bank series contain at most 26 annual observations per economy over 1995--2020. This yields fewer temporal training windows and makes estimates more sensitive to individual years and structural breaks. The comparison is also confounded by panel composition (203 heterogeneous global economies versus 38 European destinations) and by different test periods: the World Bank test includes the 2020 pandemic shock, whereas the Eurostat test covers 2024--2026. Thus, Table~\ref{tab:benchmark_wb} does not isolate temporal frequency as the sole cause of the difference. This interpretation is consistent with tourism forecasting evidence that model rankings vary by frequency and that simple naïve forecasts can be difficult to beat on annual series \cite{athanasopoulos2011tourism}.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Main Findings}
This study highlights three primary empirical findings:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textit{Efficacy of Extended Linear LTSF:} Simple linear decomposition models, when equipped with exogenous calendar projections and regime modulation, outperform heavy recurrent architectures on macroeconomic time series.
    \item \textit{Consistency-Oriented Learning:} The coupled penalty $\mathcal{L}_{\text{cons}}$ introduces the relationship $\hat{Y}_{\text{nig}}\geq\hat{Y}_{\text{arr}}$ into the training objective, with the aim of discouraging incompatible forecast pairs before correction. The principal empirical consistency result is the full framework's raw violation rate of only \textbf{0.15\% (7/4,712 forecasts)} before the final projection; 99.85\% of forecasts already satisfy this check. The subsequent 0.00\% rate is guaranteed by the clipping operation and is therefore not an independent measure of learning quality. The raw violation rate and historical economic band deviation (16.02\%) provide the more informative empirical assessment.
    \item \textit{Reliable Distribution-Free Uncertainty:} Conformal prediction guarantees valid coverage without relying on rigid Gaussian assumptions.
\end{enumerate}

The penalty and projection serve different purposes: $\mathcal{L}_{\text{cons}}$ is formulated to discourage violations during learning, while $\Pi_{\mathcal{C}}$ corrects the remaining infeasible outputs at inference. Assessing the penalty's separate contribution requires computing it on unprojected neural outputs and comparing raw violation rates in a matched loss-removal ablation; the reported 0.15\% rate characterizes the complete framework before its final projection (Tables~\ref{tab:ablation} and~\ref{tab:projection_effect}).

\noindent \textbf{Component Interpretations:}
The following mechanisms reflect the architectural intent of the framework; individual contributions should be weighed against ablation findings:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RA-Exo-DLinear:} Incorporates known future calendar features and historical macro shocks alongside target series.
    \item \textbf{HMM + FiLM:} Rescales trend representations according to market states identified by HMM.
    \item \textbf{TRMF:} Captures shared latent cross-country co-movements across destinations.
    \item \textbf{Coupled Loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{cons}}$:} Is designed to discourage incompatible arrival and overnight-stay predictions during learning by penalizing violations in unprojected neural outputs.
    \item \textbf{Projector $\Pi_{\mathcal{C}}$:} Provides a deterministic final feasibility check, correcting the seven residual violations in the reported test set.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Practical Implications}
For destination management organizations (DMOs) and hospitality operators, the role of $\mathcal{L}_{\text{cons}}$ is to incorporate compatibility between arrivals and overnight stays into model fitting, so that consistency is considered when forecasts are produced. The relevant operational result is the limited need for final correction: the full framework has a raw violation rate of only \textbf{0.15\% (7/4,712 forecasts)}, leaving 99.85\% compliant before the final projection. The projector then handles the residual cases as a safeguard. RA-HE-DLinear also provides multi-horizon forecasts and feature attribution insights, with training completed in under 40 seconds in the evaluated environment.

Operational monitoring should retain forecasts before and after projection and report the raw violation rate, correction magnitude, WAPE/MAE, and historical economic band deviation. Tracking these measures reveals whether the system increasingly relies on correction as market conditions change and whether compatible predictions also remain accurate. This provides a more informative basis for capacity planning and resource allocation than reporting the enforced post-projection feasibility rate alone.

\subsection{Study Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
We note three limitations warranting further study:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textit{Annual Data Sparsity and Decomposition Scale:} The World Bank series contain at most 26 annual observations per economy over 1995--2020, with fewer observations available for training and few distinct disruption--recovery episodes. Pooling countries can support estimation but does not restore missing monthly or quarterly dynamics. The current annual implementation retains the moving-average default $K_{\text{ma}}=13$ with a four-year input window ($L=4$, $H=1$). At annual frequency, this kernel nominally spans 13 years, although only four observed years enter each window; the remaining support is supplied by repeated endpoint values. The resulting trend estimate depends heavily on padding and may smooth abrupt changes. Its residual should therefore be interpreted as interannual variation, not within-year seasonality. Annual models need a separate comparison of short kernels, such as $K_{\text{ma}}=3$, and a baseline without decomposition, with settings selected through chronological validation and evaluated on held-out disruption and recovery periods. Until this comparison is completed, the two-panel results support evaluation at different frequencies but do not establish that the decomposition generalizes reliably across temporal resolutions.
    \item \textit{Exchangeability in Conformal Prediction:} Split conformal assumes exchangeability. During severe black-swan disruptions, this assumption may be locally challenged; future work will explore online Adaptive Conformal Inference (ACI).
    \item \textit{Publication Latency and Missing Leading Demand Signals:} The current exogenous streams describe reported prices and enacted policies rather than contemporaneous travel intentions. Full monthly HICP breakdowns are generally released in the middle of the following month \cite{eurostat_hicp_release}. Oxford Stringency originates from daily policy records \cite{hale2021oxford}, but aggregation to monthly or annual panels reduces temporal resolution; the policy reference date also does not establish when a record first became available to a forecaster. These inputs therefore do not ensure advance warning of abrupt changes in search or booking behavior. Without such demand signals, regime conditioning may respond only after a turning point becomes visible in the observed series. Future work should test daily or weekly Google Trends search-interest indices, which are normalized measures rather than absolute search counts \cite{google_trends_data}, alongside advance airline booking or ticket-sales snapshots, including IATA/ARC sources where coverage and delivery timing are suitable \cite{iata2025dds}. Integration must respect publication timestamps and use only searches and bookings known at each forecast origin. Evaluation should compare the existing covariates with the additional streams through rolling forecast origins and archived data versions, measuring WAPE/MAE, turning-point detection delay, and false alarms during disruption and recovery periods. Earlier detection remains a hypothesis for further evaluation, not an established result of the current experiments.
\end{enumerate}

The forecasting objective is to remain useful during changing market conditions, conditional on information available at the forecast origin. Regime conditioning can support adaptation once a disruption is reflected in observed demand or covariates, but a short annual history cannot identify the timing and magnitude of an unprecedented event in advance. Realized policy or health indicators observed only after an event begins must not be treated as known predictors in an earlier forecast. Validation should therefore distinguish forecasts made before a shock from updates produced after signals become observable; mixed-frequency indicators and online updating are directions for further evaluation, rather than demonstrated solutions in the present annual experiment.

\section{Conclusion}
This study proposed \textbf{RA-HE-DLinear}, a spatial-temporal linear decomposition framework designed to address three core limitations of LTSF-Linear architectures in multi-country tourism demand forecasting. The framework integrates the regime-conditioned RA-Exo-DLinear temporal engine, low-rank TRMF cross-country spillover modeling, an asymmetric anchor consistency solver, and deep conformal uncertainty calibration.

On the Eurostat monthly panel, RA-HE-DLinear achieves a WAPE of $14.59\% \pm 0.46\%$ on arrivals and $25.00\% \pm 0.54\%$ on overnight stays. On the World Bank annual panel, corresponding WAPEs are $9.45\% \pm 0.38\%$ and $28.27\% \pm 0.82\%$. The anchor projection eliminates all 7 physical constraint violations in the Eurostat test set (0.15\% intervention rate). Component ablation confirms the contribution of each module within the evaluated benchmarks.

Future investigations will assess generalization across additional panels, examine temporal stability over longer horizons, and explore the integration of real-time mobility streams.
