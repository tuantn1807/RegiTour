\begin{abstract}
Multi-country tourism demand forecasting is challenging due to temporal heterogeneity, economic fluctuations, and cross-country dependencies. This paper proposes RA-HE-DLinear, a lightweight regime-aware framework for jointly forecasting tourist arrivals and overnight stays. It combines DLinear with causal HMM-conditioned soft-gated FiLM to capture regime-dependent trends, incorporates macroeconomic and calendar signals, and uses Temporal Regularized Matrix Factorization (TRMF) to model cross-country dependencies. Forecast integration, consistency adjustment, uncertainty quantification, and TreeSHAP-based feature analysis enhance the model's interpretability. Experiments on Eurostat data covering 38 destinations and World Bank data covering 203 economies show WAPE values of 9.16\% for arrivals and 9.64\% for overnight stays, compared with 9.36\% and 10.47\% for Vanilla DLinear. The framework also reduces pandemic-period arrivals WAPE from 76.98\% to 66.69\% and achieves 9.45\% arrivals WAPE on the World Bank dataset, outperforming Persistence (29.91\%) and DLinear (54.93\%).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Tourism demand forecasting plays an important role in destination management, accommodation planning, and transport operations \cite{song2019tourism,clements1998forecasting}. Tourist arrivals and overnight stays are two complementary indicators of tourism activity. While arrivals reflect visitor traffic at accommodation establishments, overnight stays represent the overall utilization of accommodation services. Together, these indicators provide insights into tourism market activity and help assess the average length of stay (LOS), calculated as the ratio of overnight stays to arrivals.

Forecasting tourism demand across multiple countries is challenging because tourism markets are influenced by changing economic conditions, external shocks, and interrelated demand patterns. Events such as the COVID-19 pandemic \cite{gossling2020pandemics}, along with fluctuations in energy prices and inflation, can substantially alter tourism demand over time. Furthermore, tourism markets across countries may exhibit shared trends and regional dependencies \cite{yang2019spatial,cang2014comparative}, making it difficult for models that treat each country independently to capture broader market dynamics. Another important consideration is the consistency between arrivals and overnight stays. Forecasts generated independently may produce unrealistic relationships between these indicators, reducing their usefulness for operational planning. In addition, changes in tourism demand patterns over time make reliable uncertainty estimation difficult \cite{barber2023conformal,gibbs2021adaptive}.

Recent developments in time-series forecasting have demonstrated the effectiveness of lightweight linear models, particularly DLinear \cite{zeng2023dlinear}, which offer a computationally efficient alternative to more complex deep learning architectures. Despite their advantages, conventional linear forecasting models do not explicitly account for changing demand conditions, economic influences, or dependencies across countries. However, jointly modeling tourism demand dynamics, economic conditions, and cross-country dependencies while ensuring forecast consistency and uncertainty estimation remains challenging. This motivates a unified framework for multi-country tourism demand forecasting.

To address this gap, we propose RA-HE-DLinear (Regime-Aware Hierarchical-Economic DLinear), a lightweight framework for multi-country tourism demand forecasting. The proposed framework combines information about changing tourism demand conditions, economic indicators, and cross-country relationships to improve the representation of tourism market dynamics. It also incorporates mechanisms to maintain consistency between arrivals and overnight stays and to quantify forecast uncertainty. In addition, we examine the contributions of economic variables to model predictions through feature attribution and sensitivity analysis, providing further insights into the factors associated with tourism demand.

The main contributions of this study are threefold. First, we develop a regime-aware forecasting framework that integrates economic information and cross-country dependencies into a lightweight time-series model. Second, we incorporate forecast integration, operational consistency, and uncertainty estimation to support more coherent and informative tourism demand forecasts. Third, we conduct an empirical evaluation using multi-country tourism data and examine the role of economic variables in the resulting predictions. The technical details of the proposed framework are presented in Section 3.


\section{Related Work}
Research on tourism demand forecasting has explored various approaches to modeling temporal patterns, market dynamics, and relationships across destinations. However, several challenges remain in jointly addressing regime changes, cross-country dependencies, forecast consistency, and uncertainty.

Time-Series and Tourism Forecasting. Tourism forecasting has evolved from statistical and econometric methods, such as SARIMA and ETS \cite{song2019tourism,athanasopoulos2011tourism}, to machine learning approaches such as LightGBM \cite{ke2017lightgbm} and deep learning models, including LSTM and Transformer-based architectures \cite{law2019tourism,zhou2021informer,nie2023patchtst,wu2023timesnet,liu2024itransformer}. Although these methods offer different capabilities for capturing nonlinear and temporal patterns, their application does not necessarily address changing market regimes and cross-country dependencies simultaneously. Meanwhile, DLinear \cite{zeng2023dlinear} has demonstrated competitive performance on several long-term forecasting benchmarks, providing a lightweight alternative to more complex architectures. However, its original formulation does not explicitly incorporate regime information or cross-country relationships.

Regime Shifts and Spatial Dependencies. Markov-switching models have been applied to capture changes in tourism demand under different market conditions \cite{hamilton1989regime,yao2020neural,botha2022forecasting}, while FiLM \cite{perez2018film} provides a mechanism for conditioning neural representations on contextual information. Separately, spatial econometric models \cite{yang2019spatial,jiao2020local} typically rely on predefined spatial structures to represent relationships across destinations, whereas TRMF \cite{yu2016trmf} learns latent temporal factors from multivariate time-series data. Although these approaches address different aspects of changing demand and cross-series dependencies, integrating regime-aware modeling with data-driven cross-country representations remains a relevant challenge for multi-country tourism forecasting.

Forecast Consistency and Uncertainty. Forecast reconciliation methods \cite{kourentzes2019coherent} aim to maintain coherence among related forecasts, particularly in hierarchical and grouped structures. However, the relationship between tourist arrivals and overnight stays involves an operational inequality constraint rather than conventional additive aggregation. This distinction motivates consistency mechanisms tailored to tourism indicators. For uncertainty estimation, split and adaptive conformal prediction methods \cite{vovk2005algorithmic,angelopoulos2021conformal,barber2023conformal,gibbs2021adaptive} provide model-agnostic prediction intervals. Nevertheless, temporal distribution shifts may affect their coverage, highlighting the need to evaluate uncertainty estimates under changing tourism demand conditions.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

\subsection{Problem Formulation}
For destination entity $i$ at time $t$, let $\mathbf{y}_{i,t}=[Y^{\text{arr}}_{i,t},Y^{\text{nig}}_{i,t}]^T$ denote tourist arrivals and overnight stays. The average Length of Stay (LOS) is formally defined as the ratio of overnight stays to arrivals:
\begin{equation}
\text{LOS}_{i,t} = \frac{Y^{\text{nig}}_{i,t}}{Y^{\text{arr}}_{i,t}}.
\label{eq:los_definition}
\end{equation}
Under UNWTO international statistical standards \cite{unwto2010recommendations}, visitors registered at tourist accommodation establishments by definition stay for at least one night; same-day trips (duration zero) are excluded from accommodation counts. Consequently, physical and operational validity requires a universal lower bound $\text{LOS}_{\min}=1.0$. Given lookback horizon $L$, past macroeconomic covariates $\mathbf{X}^{\text{past}}$, future calendar features $\mathbf{x}^{\text{fut}}$, and origin regime beliefs, we predict $H$ future steps satisfying:
\begin{equation}
\hat{Y}_{i, \tau}^{\text{nig}} \ge \hat{Y}_{i, \tau}^{\text{arr}} \times \text{LOS}_{\min}, \quad \forall \tau \in \{t+1, \dots, t+H\}.
\label{eq:physical_floor}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Regime-Aware Temporal Core (RA-Exo-DLinear)}
\textbf{Causal HMM Conditioning.} Following Markov regime switching \cite{hamilton1989regime}, a three-state Gaussian HMM is fitted on training aggregate YoY arrivals growth, COVID stringency \cite{hale2021oxford}, and HICP lodging inflation \cite{eurostat_hicp_release}. Forward-filtered regime beliefs at forecast origin $o$ are computed causally without lookahead:
\begin{equation}
\alpha_t^{(o)}(k) \propto p\big(\mathbf{u}_t^{(o)} \mid S_t=k\big) \sum_{j=0}^{K-1}\alpha_{t-1}^{(o)}(j)A_{j,k}, \quad \hat{s}_o = \operatorname*{arg\,max}_{k} \alpha_o^{(o)}(k),
\label{eq:hmm_forward}
\end{equation}
mapping the dominant state $\hat{s}_o \in \{0,1,2\}$ (normal, crisis, recovery) to an embedding $\mathbf{e}_r = \mathbf{E}_{\hat{s}_o,:} \in \mathbb{R}^{8}$.

\textbf{Linear Decomposition and Soft-Gated FiLM.} Each channel is decomposed via a centered moving average ($K_{\text{ma}}=13$) \cite{zeng2023dlinear}: $\mathbf{X}_{\text{trend}} = \operatorname{AvgPool}(\mathbf{X}, K_{\text{ma}})$ and $\mathbf{X}_{\text{seasonal}} = \mathbf{X} - \mathbf{X}_{\text{trend}}$. Projections $\mathbf{W}_T, \mathbf{W}_S \in \mathbb{R}^{H\times L}$ are initialized to last-step persistence and seasonal-lag replication. With base trend $\mathbf{z}_{T,k}=\mathbf{W}_T\mathbf{X}_{\text{trend},k}$, target-specific soft-gated FiLM \cite{perez2018film} modulates the trend using gate $g_{r,k}=\sigma(\mathbf{w}_{g,k}^T\mathbf{e}_r+b_{g,k})$. With weights initialized to zero, to constrain initial regime modulation to a conservative baseline floor $g_0 \approx 4.74\%$, the bias is computed via the logit link $b_{g,k} = \operatorname{logit}(g_0) = \ln(g_0/(1-g_0)) = \ln(e^{-3}) = -3.0$ ($g_{r,k}=\sigma(-3.0)\approx 0.047$). This near-identity warm-start prevents uncalibrated regime embeddings from distorting the stable DLinear trend early in training while maintaining non-zero gradients for gradual optimization:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
f_{\text{trend}, k} &= \big(1 + g_{r,k} \alpha_{\text{trend}, k} \tanh(\mathbf{w}_{\gamma, k}^T \mathbf{e}_r + b_{\gamma, k})\big) \mathbf{z}_{T,k} \\
&\quad + g_{r,k} (\mathbf{w}_{\beta, k}^T \mathbf{e}_r + b_{\beta, k})\mathbf{1}_H.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:film_trend}
\end{equation}
Standardized calendar features and past macroeconomic windows generate bounded adjustments $\delta_{\text{fut}, k, h} = \alpha_{\text{fut}, k} \tanh(\mathbf{w}_{F, k}^T \mathbf{x}^{\text{fut}}_h + b_{F, k})$ and $\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\text{past}, k} = \alpha_{\text{past}, k} \tanh(\mathbf{W}_{P, k} \operatorname{vec}(\mathbf{X}^{\text{past}}) + \mathbf{b}_{P, k})$. In line with Generalized Additive Models (GAM) \cite{hastie1990gam}, these are scaled by the entity's training monthly median baseline $\mathbf{S}_{\text{base}, k}$:
\begin{equation}
\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{\text{temporal}, k} = \operatorname{ReLU}\Big(f_{\text{trend}, k} + \mathbf{W}_S \mathbf{X}_{\text{seasonal}, k} + \mathbf{S}_{\text{base}, k} \odot (\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\text{fut}, k} + \boldsymbol{\delta}_{\text{past}, k})\Big).
\label{eq:temporal_forecast}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Cross-Country TRMF and Adaptive Gating}
\label{sec:trmf}
To capture transnational co-movement, we standardize country-level training matrices $\widetilde{Y}_{k,i,t} = (Y_{k,i,t}-\mu_{k,i})/(\sigma_{k,i}+10^{-5})$ and factorize $\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_k \approx \mathbf{W}_k\mathbf{X}_k^T$ with rank $R=4$ and autoregressive lag set $\mathcal{L}=\{1,2,12\}$ (monthly) or $\{1,2\}$ (annual) \cite{yu2016trmf}:
\begin{equation}
\min_{\mathbf{W}_k, \mathbf{X}_k, \boldsymbol{\Theta}_k} \|\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_k - \mathbf{W}_k \mathbf{X}_k^T\|_F^2 + \lambda_w \|\mathbf{W}_k\|_F^2 + \lambda_x \mathcal{R}_{\text{AR}}(\mathbf{X}_k, \boldsymbol{\Theta}_k).
\label{eq:trmf_objective}
\end{equation}
Extrapolated country factors are rescaled and allocated to accommodation category $c$ via historical training shares $s_{k,i,c}$ to produce $\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{\text{spatial}}$. Following mixture-of-experts gating principles \cite{jacobs1991adaptive,shazeer2017moe}, temporal and spatial forecasts are fused via validation gating:
\begin{equation}
w_{\text{time}}^{\text{adapt}} = \begin{cases}
\hat{w}, & \text{if } \mathcal{L}_{\text{val}}(\hat{w}) < \mathcal{L}_{\text{val}}(1.0) - \epsilon, \\
1.0, & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\label{eq:adaptive_fallback}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{\text{fused}} = w_{\text{time}} \hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{\text{temporal}} + (1 - w_{\text{time}}) \hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{\text{spatial}}$ ($\epsilon=10^{-5}$). When spatial blending fails to strictly reduce validation WAPE, the gate falls back to the temporal core ($w_{\text{time}}^{\text{adapt}}=1.0$). This fallback is economically critical during tranquil recovery regimes: it prevents divergent national trajectories from suffering negative spatial error spillover, while preserving cross-country stabilization during synchronized systemic crises.

\subsection{Consistency Projection \texorpdfstring{($\Pi_{\mathcal{C}}$)}{(Pi\_C)} and Conformal Calibration}
Modulation heads are trained with coupled normalized loss penalizing $\text{LOS} < 1.0$ violations ($\lambda_{\text{cons}}=0.2$) \cite{kourentzes2019coherent}:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \sum_{k \in \{\text{arr}, \text{nig}\}} \left\|\frac{\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_k - \mathbf{Y}_k}{\mathbf{S}_{\text{base}, k}}\right\|_2^2 + \lambda_{\text{cons}} \left\|\frac{\operatorname{ReLU}(\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{\text{arr}} - \hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{\text{nig}})}{\mathbf{S}_{\text{base}, \text{arr}}}\right\|_2^2.
\label{eq:coupled_loss}
\end{equation}
At inference, the arrivals-anchored projection operator $\Pi_{\mathcal{C}}$ guarantees physical validity while keeping border-verified arrivals invariant:
\begin{equation}
\Pi_{\mathcal{C}}(\hat{Y}_{\text{arr}}, \hat{Y}_{\text{nig}}) = \big(\hat{Y}_{\text{arr}}, \, \max(\hat{Y}_{\text{nig}}, \, \hat{Y}_{\text{arr}} \times 1.0)\big).
\label{eq:projection}
\end{equation}
Finally, split conformal calibration \cite{vovk2005algorithmic,angelopoulos2021conformal,barber2023conformal} computes absolute residuals $s_{i,k}=|y_{i,k}-\hat{Y}_{i,k}^*|$ on held-out calibration pairs to form $1-\alpha$ ($\alpha=0.10$) empirical intervals $\hat{\mathcal{I}}_{1-\alpha,k}=[\max(0,\hat{Y}_k^*-\hat{Q}_{1-\alpha,k}),\hat{Y}_k^*+\hat{Q}_{1-\alpha,k}]$ using quantile $\lceil(N_{\text{cal}}+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil/N_{\text{cal}}$. Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} illustrates the complete pipeline.

\subsection{Feature Attribution and Explainability (XAI)}
\label{sec:method_xai}
To interpret how macroeconomic and calendar signals govern demand predictions, we integrate post-hoc Shapley feature attribution \cite{lundberg2017shap}. The attribution $\phi_j$ for feature $j$ across feature set $\mathcal{F}$ is:
\begin{equation}
\phi_j = \sum_{S \subseteq \mathcal{F} \setminus \{j\}} \frac{|S|!(|\mathcal{F}| - |S| - 1)!}{|\mathcal{F}|!} \Big[f_{\text{att}}(S \cup \{j\}) - f_{\text{att}}(S)\Big],
\label{eq:shap_attribution}
\end{equation}
evaluated via TreeSHAP \cite{lundberg2020treeshap} on gradient-boosted surrogates approximating the temporal core. To inform policy planning, we also conduct counterfactual elasticity simulations $\mathcal{E}_j = \frac{\Delta \hat{Y}/\hat{Y}}{\Delta X_j/X_j}$ under targeted price and health shocks.

\section{Experimental Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Datasets and Experimental Setup}
We evaluate on two multi-country panels (Table~\ref{tab:datasets}): (1) \textbf{Eurostat Monthly Panel} \cite{eurostat_tourism_release} covering 38 European destinations across 4 accommodation categories (152 series, Jan.\ 2010--Jul.\ 2026, 30,248 grid rows, 4,522 observed positive test pairs in Jan.\ 2024--Jul.\ 2026); and (2) \textbf{World Bank Annual Panel} covering 203 economies (1995--2020, 4,683 rows, 497 test pairs in 2018--2020). Models share identical chronological splits without lookahead. Baselines span four families: classical statistical (Seasonal Naive, ETS, SARIMA $(1,0,0)(1,0,0)_{12}$, SARIMAX), gradient boosting (LightGBM, 200 trees) \cite{ke2017lightgbm}, modern deep Transformers (TimesNet \cite{wu2023timesnet}, PatchTST \cite{nie2023patchtst}, iTransformer \cite{liu2024itransformer}), and linear LTSF (Vanilla NLinear, DLinear \cite{zeng2023dlinear}). RA-HE-DLinear trains with AdamW ($\text{lr}=10^{-4}$, batch size 256, 5 epochs). Metrics are WAPE ($100\sum|\hat{y}-y|/\sum y$), MAE in original counts, and Physical Violation Rate ($\operatorname{PVR} = \frac{100\%}{N_{\text{pairs}}}\sum \mathbb{I}(\hat{Y}_k^{\text{nig}} < \hat{Y}_k^{\text{arr}})$).

\subsection{Comparative Performance on Eurostat}
Table~\ref{tab:reviewer_benchmark} reports Eurostat test results. Four key findings emerge:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Superiority Over Deep Transformers and Lightweight Efficiency:} With only \textbf{4,770 total parameters} (4,170 trainable modulation parameters, $\sim$2.8s runtime), \textbf{RA-HE-DLinear (Adapt.+$\Pi_{\mathcal{C}}$)} is \textbf{$21.5\times$ to $30.3\times$ smaller} than iTransformer (102.5k params, 10.5s), PatchTST (106.4k, 14.7s), and TimesNet (144.3k, 32.9s), yet achieves the highest accuracy among all learned models (\textbf{9.16\%} arrivals and \textbf{9.64\%} nights WAPE vs.\ 22.93\%/22.43\% for iTransformer, 27.55\%/26.33\% for PatchTST, and 32.45\%/33.71\% for TimesNet). Self-attention overfits post-pandemic scale shifts on log-transformed monthly series, whereas our regime-modulated linear decomposition preserves temporal fidelity and statistically outperforms Vanilla DLinear (9.36\% / 10.47\%; paired $t=3.14, p=0.0017$ on nights; Wilcoxon $p<10^{-5}$ on arrivals and $p<10^{-14}$ on nights), while Seasonal Naive (6.04\% / 5.68\%) reflects strong annual seasonal persistence during tranquil post-recovery periods.
    \item \textbf{100\% Elimination of Physical Violations:} Unconstrained baselines routinely predict fewer nights than arrivals ($\operatorname{PVR}$ of 8.786\% for SARIMAX, 2.865\% for TimesNet, 2.504\% for ETS, 2.419\% for LightGBM, 1.931\% for DLinear, 1.188\% for PatchTST, and 0.085\% for Seasonal Naive). RA-HE-DLinear achieves \textbf{0.000\% PVR}, resolving all 106 raw violations and improving nights WAPE from 9.67\% to 9.64\%.
    \item \textbf{Resilience to Econometric Divergence and Reporting Lags:} Under post-COVID inflation surges, 233/304 SARIMAX fits failed convergence and 23 converged series suffered exponential log-inverse divergence (160.60\% arrivals WAPE). Conversely, RA-HE-DLinear's bounded $\tanh$ streams remain stable, with simulated two-month reporting lags increasing WAPE by only +0.28 pp (arrivals) and +0.25 pp (nights).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Regime Dynamics, Income Tiers, and LOS}
Across four chronological phases, monthly log-growth standard deviation of arrivals surged nearly sixfold from 0.244 (pre-pandemic 2018--2019) to 1.371 (pandemic 2020--2021) before moderating to 0.443 (recovery 2022--2023) and 0.380 (recent 2024--2026), with arrivals--nights growth correlation remaining high ($r=0.933$--$0.962$).

As shown in Table~\ref{tab:reviewer_regimes}, during the 2020--2021 pandemic shock, cross-country TRMF pooling (\textbf{RA-HE Static}) reduced arrivals WAPE from DLinear's 76.98\% to \textbf{66.69\%} and nights WAPE from 71.45\% to \textbf{63.06\%}, while lowering pandemic LOS RMSE from 9.394 (with 82 undefined zero-denominator predictions) to \textbf{1.253} (0 undefined). In tranquil periods, where static spatial pooling over-smooths local recovery paths (11.62\% / 13.56\%), the adaptive validation gate sets $w_{\text{time}}^*\to 1.0$, securing 9.16\% / 9.64\% WAPE and lowering recent LOS MAE to \textbf{0.167} nights/arrival (RMSE 0.342) vs.\ DLinear's 0.182 (RMSE 0.435). Split-conformal calibration closely approaches the 90\% nominal target, achieving \textbf{86.86\%} coverage for arrivals and \textbf{87.20\%} for overnight stays under post-2023 temporal shift.

Disaggregating by World Bank FY2024 income tiers \cite{worldbank_income_fy24} shows that while gains in 30 high-income European markets are steady (8.53\% vs.\ DLinear's 8.64\% on arrivals; 9.23\% vs.\ 10.00\% on nights across 914 pairs), RA-HE-DLinear delivers substantial error reductions in the 7 higher-volatility upper-middle-income destinations (208 pairs), cutting arrivals WAPE by \textbf{3.92 pp} (from 17.39\% to \textbf{13.47\%}) and nights WAPE by \textbf{5.50 pp} (from 17.87\% to \textbf{12.37\%}).

\subsection{Ablation Study and Global Benchmark}
Table~\ref{tab:compact_ablation} summarizes the Eurostat ablation ladder and 3-seed $2^3$ factorial effects alongside the global World Bank benchmark. Exogenous projections ($E$) yield the largest factorial WAPE reduction ($-1.75\pm0.74$ pp arrivals, $-2.59\pm0.61$ pp nights), and HMM regime conditioning ($H$) adds steady gains ($-0.14\pm0.05$ pp arrivals, $-0.12\pm0.18$ pp nights). Applying ungated multiplicative FiLM ($F, g_r=1$) degrades WAPE by $+3.37\pm1.51$ pp and $+5.69\pm2.13$ pp, directly justifying our soft-gating mechanism ($b_g=-3.0$).

\subsection{Explainable AI and Policy Sensitivity Analysis}
\label{sec:results_xai}
Across exogenous drivers, \textbf{lodging inflation (HICP)} exhibits the largest explanatory share (\textbf{37.88\%}, mean $|\text{SHAP}|=284.7\text{k}$), followed by \textbf{health stringency} (\textbf{28.07\%}, $210.9\text{k}$), \textbf{weekend days} (\textbf{19.67\%}), and \textbf{working days} (\textbf{14.39\%}). Counterfactual sensitivity simulations under $\pm 5\%$ and $\pm 10\%$ HICP price shocks across major European hotel markets (France, Spain, Italy, and Germany) reveal symmetric negative price elasticity: a $+10\%$ lodging price surge contracts predicted hotel arrivals by \textbf{$-7.20\%$} and overnight stays by \textbf{$-7.56\%$} ($\mp 3.60\%$ and $\mp 3.78\%$ for $\pm 5\%$ shocks), enabling destination authorities to quantify inflation-induced demand contraction alongside 90\% conformal downside-risk margins.

\section{Decision Support, System Deployment, and Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
\textbf{Destination Cases.} Figure~\ref{fig:tourism_cases} illustrates decision support across three recovery terciles (2023/2019 arrivals ratio): (1) \textbf{Cyprus} (lagging recovery, 0.98): RA-HE-DLinear reduces arrivals/nights WAPE from DLinear's 17.05\%/20.01\% to \textbf{13.31\%/13.93\%}; (2) \textbf{Sweden} (moderate recovery, 1.03): errors drop from 8.00\%/8.19\% to \textbf{6.13\%/5.89\%} (LOS MAE 0.043); and (3) \textbf{Denmark} (rapid expansion, 1.14): errors drop from 9.38\%/11.25\% to \textbf{7.63\%/9.13\%}.

\textbf{System Deployment.} To operationalize these capabilities, the forecasting pipeline, TreeSHAP attributions, and counterfactual policy simulations are integrated into an interactive web-based \textit{RegiTour Intelligent Tourism Monitoring Dashboard} and enterprise FastAPI service for destination management organizations.

\textbf{Conclusion.} \textbf{RA-HE-DLinear} couples linear sequence decomposition with causal HMM regime conditioning, soft-gated FiLM, TRMF cross-country factorization, validation-guarded adaptive/simplex fusion, and physical projection $\Pi_{\mathcal{C}}$. Evaluated across 38 European destinations and 203 global economies, it outperforms deep Transformers ($2.4\times$--$3.5\times$ lower WAPE with $22\times$--$30\times$ fewer parameters) and Seasonal Naive, mitigates crisis regime collapse, guarantees 0.000\% physical LOS violations, and delivers explainable conformal decision support.
