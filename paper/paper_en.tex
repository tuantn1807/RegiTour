\author{Nguyen-Tuan~Tran,
        Hoai-Bao~Phan}

\IEEEtitleabstractindextext{%
\begin{abstract}
We evaluate RA-HE-DLinear for joint forecasting of tourism arrivals and overnight stays. The framework combines a decomposition-linear temporal model, HMM-conditioned FiLM, calendar and macroeconomic inputs, TRMF spatial factors, validation-guarded fusion, and a consistency projection. On \ReviewerObservedPairs{} observed Eurostat test pairs from 152 destination--accommodation series, Seasonal Naive has the lowest WAPE (\ReviewerSNaiveArrivalWAPE{}\% arrivals and \ReviewerSNaiveNightsWAPE{}\% nights). Adaptive RA-HE-DLinear achieves \ReviewerArrivalWAPE{}\% and \ReviewerNightsWAPE{}\%, compared with \ReviewerDLinearArrivalWAPE{}\% and \ReviewerDLinearNightsWAPE{}\% for DLinear, with zero post-projection violations. Three-seed factorial controls find lower error from exogenous inputs, small HMM effects, and higher error from ungated multiplicative FiLM. In the Eurostat pandemic fold, the static blend lowers arrivals WAPE from 76.98\% to 66.69\%. Annual World Bank results extend the evaluation to 203 economies, with partially imputed nights targets. A publication-lag sensitivity increases adaptive-model WAPE by 0.28 and 0.25 percentage points. Historical vintage metadata remain incomplete, and the static blend's nominal 90\% intervals attain \ReviewerArrivalCoverage{}\% and \ReviewerNightsCoverage{}\% coverage.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{F}{orecasts} of tourist arrivals and overnight stays support accommodation planning, staffing, and destination management \cite{song2019tourism,clements1998forecasting}. These targets share seasonal and economic drivers, while disruptions such as COVID-19 can alter their relationship \cite{gossling2020pandemics}. Joint forecasts therefore require both accurate counts and explicit consistency checks.

DLinear offers a parsimonious baseline by projecting moving-average trend and seasonal components \cite{zeng2023dlinear}. We extend it to multi-country panels with three mechanisms:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item An HMM-conditioned temporal engine with soft-gated FiLM and separate projections of future calendar features and past macroeconomic signals.
    \item TRMF spatial factors combined with the temporal forecast through validation-selected fusion and a temporal fallback.
    \item An arrivals-anchored projection enforcing a one-night lower bound, followed by residual-conformal uncertainty calibration.
\end{enumerate}
The resulting RA-HE-DLinear framework is evaluated against statistical and machine-learning baselines on monthly Eurostat and annual World Bank panels. Factorial controls, calendar stress folds, income groups, and length-of-stay (LOS) errors examine component contributions and destination heterogeneity. A separate publication-lag sensitivity assesses the effect of unavailable recent inputs.

\section{Related Work}
Tourism forecasting research spans seasonal and econometric models \cite{song2019tourism,athanasopoulos2011tourism}, machine-learning combinations \cite{claveria2016tourism}, and recurrent neural networks \cite{hochreiter1997lstm,law2019tourism}. LightGBM \cite{ke2017lightgbm} and CatBoost \cite{dorogush2018catboost} provide tabular alternatives. Transformer architectures \cite{zhou2021informer,wu2021autoformer,nie2023patchtst,wu2023timesnet,liu2024itransformer} offer richer temporal representations, while DLinear demonstrates the competitiveness of linear decomposition \cite{zeng2023dlinear}.

Our conditioning mechanism builds on latent economic regimes \cite{hamilton1989regime} and FiLM \cite{perez2018film}. TRMF models shared temporal factors \cite{yu2016trmf}, and gating combines expert forecasts \cite{jacobs1991adaptive,shazeer2017moe}. Tourism forecast reconciliation has addressed geographic and temporal coherence \cite{kourentzes2019coherent}. Here we examine a cross-target lower bound alongside split-conformal intervals, whose coverage guarantees require exchangeability \cite{vovk2005algorithmic,angelopoulos2021conformal}.

\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Problem Formulation}
For entity $i$, let $\mathbf{y}_{i,t}=[Y^{\text{arr}}_{i,t},Y^{\text{nig}}_{i,t}]^T$ denote arrivals and nights. We predict $H$ future periods from $L$ past target observations, past macroeconomic indicators, known future calendar features, and the origin-specific regime belief. Paired predictions satisfy
\begin{equation}
\hat{Y}_{i, \tau}^{\text{nig}} \ge \hat{Y}_{i, \tau}^{\text{arr}} \times \text{LOS}_{\min}, \quad \forall \tau \in \{t+1, \dots, t+H\},
\label{eq:physical_floor}
\end{equation}
where $\text{LOS}_{\min}=1.0$ night is the imposed accommodation consistency floor \cite{unwto2010recommendations}.

\subsection{Regime-Aware Temporal Engine (RA-Exo-DLinear)}
\label{sec:regime}
\noindent \textbf{HMM conditioning.} A three-state Gaussian HMM uses aggregate year-over-year arrivals growth, COVID stringency \cite{hale2021oxford}, and accommodation prices \cite{eurostat_hicp_release}. Signal scalers and HMM parameters are fitted on training data and frozen. For inputs admitted at origin $o$, forward-filtered beliefs are
\begin{equation}
\alpha_t^{(o)}(k) \propto p\big(\mathbf{u}_t^{(o)} \mid S_t=k\big) \sum_{j=0}^{K-1}\alpha_{t-1}^{(o)}(j)A_{j,k},
\label{eq:hmm_forward}
\end{equation}
normalized to sum to one. IDs 1, 0, and 2 rank low, middle, and high fitted training-growth centroids within each fold. The most probable origin state selects an eight-dimensional embedding
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{e}_r = \mathbf{E}_{\hat{s}_o, :} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_r}, \qquad
\hat{s}_o = \operatorname*{arg\,max}_{k \in \{0,\dots,K-1\}} \alpha_o^{(o)}(k).
\label{eq:regime_embedding}
\end{equation}

\noindent \textbf{Decomposition and initialization.} Each target channel is split by a centered moving average with endpoint replication and monthly kernel $K_{\text{ma}}=13$ \cite{zeng2023dlinear}:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{X}_{\text{trend}} = \operatorname{AvgPool}(\mathbf{X}, K_{\text{ma}}), \quad \mathbf{X}_{\text{seasonal}} = \mathbf{X} - \mathbf{X}_{\text{trend}}.
\label{eq:decomp}
\end{equation}
The $H\times L$ projections $\mathbf{W}_T$ and $\mathbf{W}_S$ initialize to last-step persistence and seasonal-lag replication, respectively. They remain frozen in the benchmark while the enabled modulation heads are trained.

\noindent \textbf{FiLM and exogenous inputs.} With projected trend $\mathbf{z}_{T,k}=\mathbf{W}_T\mathbf{X}_{\text{trend},k}$, target-specific FiLM \cite{perez2018film} uses gate $g_{r,k}=\sigma(\mathbf{w}_{g,k}^T\mathbf{e}_r+b_{g,k})$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
f_{\text{trend}, k} &= \big(1 + g_{r,k} \tilde{\gamma}_k(\mathbf{e}_r)\big) \mathbf{z}_{T,k} + g_{r,k} \beta_k(\mathbf{e}_r)\mathbf{1}_H, \\
\tilde{\gamma}_k(\mathbf{e}_r) &= \alpha_{\text{trend}, k} \tanh(\mathbf{w}_{\gamma, k}^T \mathbf{e}_r + b_{\gamma, k}), \\
\beta_k(\mathbf{e}_r) &= \mathbf{w}_{\beta, k}^T \mathbf{e}_r + b_{\beta, k}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:film_trend}
\end{equation}
Gate weights initialize to zero and $b_{g,k}=-3.0$, giving $g_{r,k}\approx0.047$ initially.

Standardized future-calendar and past-macroeconomic inputs generate bounded target-specific adjustments:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\delta_{\text{fut}, k, h} &= \alpha_{\text{fut}, k} \tanh(\mathbf{w}_{F, k}^T \mathbf{x}^{\text{fut}}_h + b_{F, k}), \; h = 1, \dots, H, \\
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\text{past}, k} &= \alpha_{\text{past}, k} \tanh\big(\mathbf{W}_{P, k} \operatorname{vec}(\mathbf{X}^{\text{past}}) + \mathbf{b}_{P, k}\big),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:exo_streams}
\end{equation}
The future projection acts at each horizon, while $\mathbf{W}_{P,k}$ maps the flattened historical window to $H$ steps. Trainable scales $(\alpha_{\text{trend},k},\alpha_{\text{fut},k},\alpha_{\text{past},k})$ initialize to $(0.35,0.25,0.20)$, with modulation weights initialized to zero.

\noindent \textbf{Baseline scaling and synthesis.} The training-only baseline $S_{\text{base},k}(i,h)$ is the entity's median count for the forecast month, floored at one. Annual panels use the entity training median. The additive forecast combines trend, seasonality, and baseline-scaled exogenous adjustments \cite{hastie1990gam}:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{\text{temporal}, k} = \operatorname{ReLU}\Big(& \underbrace{f_{\text{trend}, k}(\mathbf{X}_{\text{trend}, k}, \mathbf{e}_r)}_{\text{Regime-modulated trend}} + \underbrace{\mathbf{W}_S \mathbf{X}_{\text{seasonal}, k}}_{\text{Seasonal cycle}} \\
&+ \underbrace{\mathbf{S}_{\text{base}, k} \odot \boldsymbol{\delta}_{\text{fut}, k}}_{\text{Future calendar effect}} + \underbrace{\mathbf{S}_{\text{base}, k} \odot \boldsymbol{\delta}_{\text{past}, k}}_{\text{Past macro shocks}}\Big).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:temporal_forecast}
\end{equation}
Disabling FiLM and exogenous projections recovers the nonnegative DLinear control.

\subsection{Cross-Country Dependency Modeling (TRMF) and Adaptive Fusion}
\label{sec:trmf}
TRMF uses aggregate accommodation training matrices for arrivals and nights. Eurostat fold dimensions are $38\times72$, $38\times96$, $38\times120$, and $38\times144$, with $203\times21$ for World Bank. After filling training gaps with training-only fallbacks, rows are standardized by their means and standard deviations:
\begin{equation}
\widetilde{Y}_{k,i,t} = \frac{Y_{k,i,t}-\mu_{k,i}}{\sigma_{k,i}+10^{-5}}, \qquad \widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_k \approx \mathbf{W}_k\mathbf{X}_k^T,
\label{eq:trmf_scaling}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathbf{W}_k\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times R}$ and $\mathbf{X}_k\in\mathbb{R}^{T_b\times R}$ are spatial loadings and temporal factors, with rank $R=4$ \cite{yu2016trmf}:
\begin{equation}
\min_{\mathbf{W}_k, \mathbf{X}_k, \boldsymbol{\Theta}_k} \|\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}_k - \mathbf{W}_k \mathbf{X}_k^T\|_F^2 + \lambda_w \|\mathbf{W}_k\|_F^2 + \lambda_x \mathcal{R}_{\text{AR}}(\mathbf{X}_k, \boldsymbol{\Theta}_k),
\label{eq:trmf_objective}
\end{equation}
The AR lag set is $\{1,2,12\}$ monthly and $\{1,2\}$ annually. Factors are extrapolated recursively as $\mathbf{x}_{k,t+h}=\sum_{l\in\mathcal{L}}\boldsymbol{\theta}_{k,l}\odot\mathbf{x}_{k,t+h-l}$ and rescaled by the training statistics. Country forecasts are allocated to category $c$ using training shares:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
s_{k,i,c} &= \frac{\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_b}y_{k,i,c,t}}{\max\!\left(1,\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_b}y_{k,i,\mathrm{total},t}\right)}, \\
\hat{y}^{\mathrm{spatial}}_{k,i,c,t+h} &= s_{k,i,c}\hat{Y}^{\mathrm{country}}_{k,i,t+h}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:trmf_category_mapping}
\end{equation}

\noindent \textbf{Spatial--temporal fusion.} Temporal and spatial forecasts are blended \cite{jacobs1991adaptive,shazeer2017moe}:
\begin{equation}
\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{\text{fused}} = w_{\text{time}} \hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{\text{temporal}} + (1 - w_{\text{time}}) \hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{\text{spatial}}.
\label{eq:convex_blend}
\end{equation}
The static blend selects a sigmoid weight on held-out validation data using regularized Brent optimization:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\theta^* &= \operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\theta \in \mathbb{R}} \left\{ \mathcal{L}_{\text{val}}\big(\sigma(\theta)\big) + \lambda \theta^2 \right\}, \\
w_{\text{time}}^* &= \sigma(\theta^*) = (1 + e^{-\theta^*})^{-1},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:static_gate}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal{L}_{\text{val}}$ averages arrivals and nights WAPE, and $\lambda=0.005$. The adaptive configuration instead searches $w\in[0,1]$ and accepts blending only when it improves on the temporal forecast:
\begin{equation}
w_{\text{time}}^{\text{adapt}} = \begin{cases}
\hat{w}, & \text{if } \mathcal{L}_{\text{val}}(\hat{w}) < \mathcal{L}_{\text{val}}(1.0) - \epsilon, \\
1.0, & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\label{eq:adaptive_fallback}
\end{equation}
where $\epsilon=10^{-5}$. Otherwise, the temporal forecast receives full weight.

\subsection{Economic Constraint Projection \texorpdfstring{($\Pi_{\mathcal{C}}$)}{(Pi\_C)}}
For $\mathcal{C}=\{(u,v)\in\mathbb{R}_+^2:v\ge u\}$, training combines normalized count loss with a penalty on lower-bound violations:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} &= \sum_{k \in \{\text{arr}, \text{nig}\}} \left\|\frac{\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_k - \mathbf{Y}_k}{\mathbf{S}_{\text{base}, k}}\right\|_2^2 \\
&\quad + \lambda_{\text{cons}} \left\|\frac{\operatorname{ReLU}(\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{\text{arr}} - \hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{\text{nig}})}{\mathbf{S}_{\text{base}, \text{arr}}}\right\|_2^2,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:coupled_loss}
\end{equation}
Division is elementwise and $\lambda_{\text{cons}}=0.2$. The inference projection anchors arrivals and raises nights when needed:
\begin{equation}
\Pi_{\mathcal{C}}(\hat{Y}_{\text{arr}}, \hat{Y}_{\text{nig}}) = \left(\hat{Y}_{\text{arr}}, \, \max\left(\hat{Y}_{\text{nig}}, \, \hat{Y}_{\text{arr}} \times 1.0\right)\right).
\label{eq:projection}
\end{equation}
This operator preserves nonnegative arrivals and enforces the lower bound on nights.

\subsection{Uncertainty Quantification (Conformal DCQF)}
\label{sec:conformal_method}
Split conformal calibration uses held-out observed pairs after projection \cite{vovk2005algorithmic,angelopoulos2021conformal}, with target-specific absolute residuals
\begin{equation}
s_{i, k} = \big|y_{i, k} - \hat{Y}_{i, k}^*\big|, \qquad k \in \{\text{arr}, \text{nig}\}.
\end{equation}
For $\alpha=0.10$, let $\hat{Q}_{1-\alpha,k}$ be the empirical quantile at level $\lceil(N_{\text{cal}}+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil/N_{\text{cal}}$. The interval is
\begin{equation}
\hat{\mathcal{I}}_{1-\alpha, k} = \left[\max\left(0, \hat{Y}_k^* - \hat{Q}_{1-\alpha, k}\right), \, \hat{Y}_k^* + \hat{Q}_{1-\alpha, k}\right].
\label{eq:conformal_interval}
\end{equation}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:pipeline} summarizes fitting and inference.

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\caption{RA-HE-DLinear Training and Inference}
\label{alg:pipeline}
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Chronological training, validation, calibration, and test partitions.
\STATE Fit signal scalers, HMM parameters, TRMF factors, category shares, and baseline medians on training data.
\STATE Initialize and freeze trend/seasonal projections. Train enabled modulation heads using Eq.~\eqref{eq:coupled_loss}.
\STATE Select the fusion weight on validation using Eq.~\eqref{eq:static_gate} or Eq.~\eqref{eq:adaptive_fallback}.
\STATE Project calibration forecasts and estimate residual quantiles.
\FOR{each forecast origin $o$}
    \STATE Filter admissible HMM inputs and form temporal and spatial forecasts.
    \STATE Fuse, project via Eq.~\eqref{eq:projection}, and construct intervals via Eq.~\eqref{eq:conformal_interval}.
\ENDFOR
\RETURN Point forecasts and prediction intervals.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Reference-Period Protocol and Point-in-Time Considerations}
\label{sec:availability}
Reference dates identify observation periods. A strict point-in-time (PIT) backtest requires the latest vintage released by issuance time $o$. For release timestamp $r_{i,j,s,v}$,
\begin{equation}
v^*(i,j,s;o)=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{v:r_{i,j,s,v}\le o}r_{i,j,s,v},
\qquad x_{i,j,s}^{(o)}=x_{i,j,s,v^*}.
\label{eq:asof_selection}
\end{equation}
This restriction applies to tourism histories, macroeconomic covariates, and HMM emissions. Future inputs are limited to known calendar schedules. Eurostat accommodation and HICP reporting delays motivate separate availability checks \cite{eurostat_tourism_release,eurostat_hicp_release}.

Table~\ref{tab:reviewer_pit} evaluates a two-month accommodation lag and a one-month HICP lag at each December origin. Unreleased tourism counts use prior-year values scaled by released August--October growth. December HICP uses November's value, and HMM beliefs propagate from the October state without later emissions. Adaptive-model arrivals/nights WAPE increases by 0.28/0.25 percentage points, compared with 0.70/0.75 for DLinear. Post-projection violations remain zero. This experiment measures sensitivity to assumed publication delays on the retained snapshot. Historical revision vintages and observation-specific release metadata remain incomplete.

\subsection{Length of Stay and Joint Target Diagnostics}
\label{sec:los_analysis}
LOS is the ratio of nights to arrivals for matching destination, accommodation category, and month. For count errors $\epsilon_A=\hat{Y}^{\text{arr}}-Y^{\text{arr}}$ and $\epsilon_N=\hat{Y}^{\text{nig}}-Y^{\text{nig}}$, positive actual and predicted arrivals give
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\operatorname{LOS}}-\operatorname{LOS}
=\frac{\epsilon_N-\operatorname{LOS}\,\epsilon_A}{\hat{Y}^{\mathrm{arr}}}.
\label{eq:los_error_decomposition}
\end{equation}
Proportional count errors can cancel, while small predicted-arrivals denominators amplify ratio error. LOS MAE and RMSE are therefore evaluated separately from count errors, with undefined ratios reported explicitly.

\input{tables/reviewer_los.tex}

On aggregate accommodation series, DLinear has 10, 82, 2, and 7 undefined ratios across the four folds. The static blend has none. Pandemic LOS RMSE falls from 9.394 for DLinear to 1.253 for the static blend, with the former strongly affected by near-zero predictions such as Iceland in March 2021.

On 1,122 recent common cases, static-blend LOS MAE/RMSE is 0.155/0.350 versus 0.182/0.435 for DLinear. Its raw and projected errors coincide to reported precision. For the adaptive model, projection corrects 22 aggregate-category violations, reducing LOS MAE from 0.184 to 0.167 and RMSE from 0.423 to 0.342. Country errors vary substantially, from static LOS MAE of 0.043 in Sweden to 0.295 in Cyprus. These ratios describe accommodation registrations and nights, with individual trip duration requiring visitor-level data.

\section{Tourism Management Implications and Discussion}
\label{sec:management}
Joint forecasts distinguish accommodation registration volumes from cumulative overnight demand. Their seasonal profiles can inform discussions of reception workloads, staffing, and maintenance. Prediction intervals provide ranges for these scenarios, while the imposed floor prevents contradictory paired forecasts.

\input{tables/reviewer_cases.tex}

Cyprus, Sweden, and Denmark represent median destinations within three pre-test recovery terciles (Table~\ref{tab:reviewer_cases} and Figure~\ref{fig:tourism_cases}). Adaptive-model arrivals WAPE falls from DLinear's 17.05\%, 8.00\%, and 9.38\% to 13.31\%, 6.13\%, and 7.63\%, respectively. Their static LOS MAEs span 0.043--0.295 nights per arrival, illustrating destination variation beyond pooled count scores.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
The study evaluates forecasts rather than operational optimization. Capacity, occupancy, costs, and service targets are needed to establish staffing or allocation gains.

Evaluation limits include annual December origins, a constant recent-test HMM state within each seed, incomplete release-vintage metadata, and partially imputed World Bank nights. The factorial assesses ungated FiLM, so a matched soft-gate ablation remains necessary to isolate the revised gate's contribution.

\section{Conclusion}
RA-HE-DLinear combines regime conditioning, exogenous inputs, spatial factors, and an arrivals-anchored projection. Adaptive fusion achieves \ReviewerArrivalWAPE{}\% arrivals and \ReviewerNightsWAPE{}\% nights WAPE on the recent Eurostat test, improving on DLinear while Seasonal Naive remains the strongest count baseline. Exogenous inputs consistently improve the factorial comparisons, whereas ungated FiLM raises errors and HMM effects are small. The static blend improves pandemic-fold accuracy, and projection eliminates lower-bound violations. Group and LOS diagnostics qualify pooled comparisons. Publication-lag sensitivity, incomplete vintage records, and semi-synthetic annual nights limit operational generalization.
